# Meetily for Android

A lightweight, privacy-first companion app to the Meetily desktop app: record meetings on your phone, get a transcript made on the phone (live as you speak, if you turn that on), take notes alongside it, and generate a summary — all stored locally on the device.

This is a standalone native Android app (Kotlin). It does **not** depend on the desktop Rust core or the archived Python backend.

## Features

- **Transcription on the phone**, with two engines:
  - **On-device** (default): Whisper, Parakeet or Nemotron running on the phone (whisper.cpp and sherpa-onnx are compiled into the app, arm64/x86_64). A new install offers a model suited to the phone on its first run (Set up on-device AI); otherwise download one in Settings → Transcription (Whisper Tiny 75 MB up to Large v3 Turbo Q 547 MB, or a ~630 MB Parakeet/Nemotron model); until then meetings are recorded as audio only and can be transcribed afterwards from the meeting's ⋮ → Check transcript accuracy. By default the transcript is made when you stop, which is more accurate and, while recording, uses about a tenth of the battery that live transcription does; turn on Settings → "Transcribe live" to see words as they are spoken.
  - **Android speech service** (opt-in): instant partial results, but Android documents it as likely to stream audio to remote servers (Google's on most phones), it keeps no audio, and switching to it asks for confirmation. On Android 12+ you can prefer the phone's offline recognizer (Settings → "Prefer on-device transcription").
  - Installs upgraded from earlier versions keep the system engine and are offered the on-device one once.
- **Speech detection before transcription** — after the existing loudness check, a small Silero VAD model scores each chunk of audio on the phone, and a chunk with no speech in it is never sent to Whisper, Parakeet or Nemotron. That means fewer recogniser passes over silence, steady fan or air-conditioning noise and typing, and less text invented from noise. It applies wherever the on-device engine transcribes: live transcription, the transcript made after a recording, imports, and accuracy checks. A chunk that is kept reaches the recogniser unchanged, so its timestamps, tap-to-seek positions and speaker labels are the same as without the detector. Settings → Transcription (on-device section) → "Skip silence with speech detection" is on by default; turning it off restores the earlier loudness-only behaviour, and is worth trying if a very quiet speaker is being missed. It is not a music filter (music can score as speech, and is then transcribed as before), and it is not always faster: the detector itself costs roughly a minute per hour of audio on a fast core, so the saving depends on how much of a recording is loud but not speech. Silero VAD v6.2 (MIT, Silero Team, in the ggml form the whisper.cpp project publishes) is built into the app (`ggml-silero-v6.2.0.bin`, 885,098 bytes, about 0.9 MB). The only other bundled models are the GTCRN speech enhancer behind Clear voice (`gtcrn_simple.onnx`, 535,638 bytes) and ML Kit's Latin text recogniser for photos; every other speech or language model is still downloaded on request, through the licence and free-space checks. On first use it is copied once into the app's private no-backup storage; it runs entirely on the phone, sends nothing and needs no network. If the copy, the library, the model or its built-in self-test fails, Recap transcribes every chunk as before and writes one "speech gate off" warning to logcat (tag `meetily_vad`). This is separate from the playback "skip silence" toggle, which works from the transcript's timings. The detector runs through whisper.cpp, in the same native library as the engines, so a Parakeet or Nemotron run loads that library for it. sherpa-onnx's own Silero VAD was looked at again after the move to sherpa-onnx v2.0.3, which fixed its long-stream crash (#4079) and its Flush sample drop (#4147), and was not adopted: it would need a second, different Silero export bundled, re-tuned thresholds and a new self-test, for no benefit found (the reasoning is in ACCELERATION_FINDINGS.md).
- **Global search** across all meetings — titles, transcripts, notes, summaries, attendees, tags, action items and their owners, text read from attached photos (whiteboards, slides), and attached file names — from the search bar on the main screen. Under each matching meeting the list shows the words around the match, with the match highlighted: after its time into the recording ("0:42:10 · …") for a transcript line, or labelled Notes, Summary, Action item or Photo text when only one of those matched. A match only in the title, tags, attendees or a file name shows no snippet. Tapping a meeting found by a transcript line opens it on the Transcript tab at that line, with its chapter expanded and the line outlined briefly; when the line's own words matched, Find in meeting opens already searching for what you typed. With TalkBack a row reads "Match at 0:42:10: …", and opening a hit moves focus to the line it landed on.
- **Find in meeting** — the search icon in the meeting page's toolbar opens a find bar over the transcript: every match is marked, "3 of 12" counts them, and previous / next step through them, wrapping round at either end and expanding collapsed chapters on the way. It ignores case, and accents in Latin, Greek and Cyrillic text ("cafe" finds "Café"). Back or the close button closes it and clears the marks. It only finds; Find & replace is a separate dialog.
- **Calendar integration** — start recording during a scheduled event and the meeting's title and guest list pre-fill from your calendar (read-only, toggleable, permission asked on first use); a "From calendar" button lets you pick the event manually.
- **Nocturne design** — the app wears the Nocturne identity from a Claude Design handoff: Inter type, a deep indigo-violet dark theme (the hero) and a "Daylight blue" light theme, depth via glow and layering. Home centers on a record orb ("Tap to record — meetings stay on this phone", or "some processing may leave this phone" when your settings send audio or text out) over a gradient hairline and RECENT MEETINGS cards with icon wells; recording gets a radial ground, a breathing orb glow, live EQ bars, a 44sp light-weight timer, and outlined pill controls; the meeting page has Summary | Transcript segmented tabs with a staggered reveal after generating; speakers appear as tinted chips throughout. A sun/moon toggle in the home header flips themes instantly (System/Light/Dark also in Settings). Every screen draws edge-to-edge, with the status and navigation bars transparent over the app background.
- **Background-safe recording** — capture and transcription run in a foreground service with an ongoing notification (Pause, Mark moment and Finish actions), so microphone recording continues when you switch apps or lock the phone. Device audio is the exception: on Android 15 and later, Android stops device-audio capture when the screen locks or times out, so keep the phone unlocked with the screen on for the whole meeting. Recap warns about this before asking for screen-capture consent, and if capture stops anyway, the notification and recording screen say so and what was captured up to then is kept when you tap Finish. The meeting is saved incrementally, so an interrupted session (crash, process kill, swipe-away) is recovered on next launch.
- **Backup & restore** — Settings → Data exports your whole library (meetings + photos + audio + voiceprints) to a single file you control — plain `.zip` or a passphrase-encrypted `.recapbak` (AES-256-GCM, PBKDF2 key derivation) — and restores from either. API keys are never included, and meeting data is excluded from Android's automatic device/cloud backups. Clear-voice copies are left out too, since they can be made again from the recording, which is backed up; after a restore the meeting plays its recording until a copy is made again.
- **Security hardening** — optional **app lock** (fingerprint / face / device PIN required to open the app after it's been in the background), optional **screenshot & recents-preview blocking** (FLAG_SECURE), and **Local-only AI** (on by default): the app refuses AI endpoints outside localhost / your LAN / Tailscale-style private ranges until you explicitly allow cloud providers, and plain-http endpoints are never allowed beyond the private network at all. Model downloads are https-only.
- **First-run onboarding with a "Set up on-device AI" step, recording-consent notice, an in-app privacy policy and Terms of use** (Settings → Privacy policy, Settings → Terms of use). The setup step suggests a transcription model and an on-device summary model that suit the phone's RAM, processor and language, and shows each one's download size and memory fit; its choices are Download both, Transcription only and Choose later. It warns before downloading over mobile data, says so when the phone is offline, and goes through the usual licence acceptance and free-space check. Choose later changes nothing. Download both turns on-device AI summaries on once the summary model has downloaded; on a phone with too little memory or free space for a summary model it offers transcription only, and the built-in offline summariser carries on. The Terms of use include a "Model use restrictions" section that incorporates the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy, the Health AI Developer Foundations terms and Prohibited Use Policy, and the Llama 3.2 and 3.1 Acceptable Use Policies.
- **Rich notes** — type plain notes during the meeting; afterwards the meeting page renders them as formatted Markdown (headings, **bold**/*italic*/`code`, bullets) with **live checklists** — tap a checkbox to toggle it right in the rendered view. An edit mode with a formatting toolbar (B / I / H / list / checklist) writes the markers for you; storage stays plain text, so exports and LLM context are unchanged.
- **File attachments** — attach any file (PDF, deck, doc…) to a meeting from the Files section; stored privately with the meeting, opened with the matching app on tap, included in backups, long-press to remove.
- **Attendees & speaker tagging** — list attendees for a meeting, then tag who said what three ways: tap an attendee **chip** while recording (that speaker sticks to everything said until you tap another), tap any transcript segment to tag it directly (tagging the latest segment also sets the sticky speaker), or use **Suggest speakers (AI)** on the meeting page to label untagged lines from conversational context via your LLM — always previewed, never overwriting manual tags. Speaker names flow into exports and summaries.
- **Auto-detect speakers (experimental)** — with the on-device engine, an on-device voice-embedding model (sherpa-onnx) groups transcript segments by who's talking, labeling unnamed voices "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2"…; tap any auto-labeled segment and choose "Name Speaker N everywhere…" to rename the whole voice at once. Enable in Settings → Transcription and download a speaker model (one-time, 26–109 MB: WeSpeaker ResNet34 is the recommended English default; the deeper WeSpeaker ResNet293 and ResNet152 are the most accurate; TitaNet large/small and CAM++ English+Chinese are also available). Saved voice profiles keep their enrollment audio, so when you switch speaker models they convert automatically on the next recording or import; only profiles saved without audio (from a live meeting's "Save voice", or before this feature) need re-enrolling — the app warns you which is which before switching. Manual tags always win over auto labels. (Design: DIARIZATION_PLAN.md.)
  - **CAM++** is now the re-export upstream published on 2026-11-19 (catalogue key `campplus-zh-en-v2`, 27 MB), whose embeddings no longer depend on the clip's length modulo 2 s (sherpa-onnx issue 4201). An install that already has the earlier CAM++ file keeps it, listed as "CAM++ (English + Chinese, earlier download)", and its voice profiles keep matching it; switching to the new entry converts profiles that have banked audio, as with any model switch. If the selected earlier file is missing (a restored backup, or a deleted file), the selection moves to the new entry at the next launch, which then needs its 27 MB download. New installs see only the new entry.
  - **Damaged speaker models** — sherpa-onnx's speaker-embedding loader still ends the process on a file it cannot use (v2.0.3), so Recap checks a speaker model's ONNX structure and metadata before loading it. A truncated or damaged file skips speaker detection, with a `speaker model <key> rejected: …` line in logcat (tag `SherpaEmbedder`), instead of closing the app. Parakeet and Nemotron files need no such check: since sherpa-onnx v2.0.0 their loaders report onnxruntime errors to the app, so a damaged file fails cleanly (an import shows "Could not load the … model") instead of closing it.
- **Check transcript accuracy** — the ⋮ menu on any meeting that kept its audio runs a second, heavier model over the recording and shows you exactly where the two passes disagree, moment by moment, with a play button on each one. Live capture has to keep up in real time and cuts the audio at silences; an unhurried second pass often hears more. "Both models agree" is a real answer here — it is a check, not an automatic rewrite. Keep either version per moment or swap the lot; the transcript is never touched until you say so, a cancelled check leaves it exactly as it was, and your speaker tags and highlights carry across to whatever you accept. A meeting recorded as audio only (before any transcription model was downloaded) is transcribed the same way. Turn on "Check the transcript after each meeting" (Settings → Transcription) and it runs on its own after every recording, skipping the pass when the battery is low and the phone is not charging. When the meeting has a clear-voice copy, the check can transcribe the copy instead, but only if you tick "Transcribe the clear-voice copy instead" (see Clear voice).
- **Flag meetings for follow-up** — tap the star on any meeting card (or use the ⋮ menu) and it stands out in the library with an accent edge; a "Flagged" chip filters the list down to just those.
- **Select several meetings** — long-press a meeting on the home list to start selecting. The header becomes a selection bar with the count, Select all, Delete and More: Add tag, Remove audio, Export as zip (Markdown), and Meeting options when exactly one meeting is selected. Tap rows to add or remove them; Back leaves selection mode; the selection survives rotation, and searching or filtering drops meetings that are no longer shown. Delete and Remove audio confirm with counts and say how many selected meetings they leave out (no audio, no transcript yet or an unfinished one, or something is working on them). Add tag adds one tag to every selected meeting. Export writes one .zip, to a place you choose in the system file picker, with a Markdown file per meeting carrying the same fixed clinical and legal notices as a single export. Meeting options is the single-meeting sheet a long-press used to open (rename, check accuracy, summarise, topics, share, flag, with Delete set apart at the end); with TalkBack it is also a "Meeting options" action on every row, rows are announced as checkable, and the count is spoken as it changes.
- **Tags & series** — give meetings comma-separated tags on the meeting page; the home screen grows a filter-chip row with your #tags plus automatically detected recurring-meeting series ("Team standup ×5", matched by title with dates/numbers stripped). Tags also boost "Ask your library" retrieval.
- **Weekly digest** — "Weekly digest" in the home-screen menu compiles your last 7 days: every meeting with its gist, open action items grouped by owner, and starred highlights — instantly and offline; with an LLM configured it upgrades in place to a written digest (themes, decisions, follow-ups, citing meetings). Shareable as text.
- **Transcript editing** — tap any line on the meeting page for "Edit text…" (fix transcription errors; clearing the text deletes the line), "Split into two…" (separate overlapping speakers: place the cursor where the second voice starts, split, then tag who said the second half — which can feed their voice profile too) and "Fix a word everywhere…" (Find & replace, starting from a word on that line). Word timings survive these edits: an edited line keeps tap-to-seek and playback follow, with only the words you changed re-timed, and both halves of a split keep theirs, the second half's Play from here starting at its first word's audio time. (Before 3.18.0-beta1, Edit text dropped a line's timings and Split dropped both halves'.)
- **Find & replace** — ⋮ → Find & replace… on the meeting page, or "Fix a word everywhere…" in a transcript line's tap menu and its TalkBack actions. Whole word is on and Match case off by default. Choose the areas (transcript lines, notes, summary, action items including their owners), each with a live count of the changes it would make; matches that already read as the replacement are not counted. Leaving Replace with empty removes the word, and a replace never empties a transcript line or an action item. Word timings are kept, so tap-to-seek and playback follow keep working on the lines it changed. There is one level of undo, from the Snackbar or ⋮ → Undo last replace while it is available: it survives rotation but not leaving the meeting, and it leaves alone anything edited since the replace, saying how many places it skipped. A replace that changes the transcript marks an existing summary "Transcript changed since this summary was written"; replacing in the summary fixes its wording but not what it paraphrased, so regenerate it for a faithful summary. After a replace that corrects a name or term (one with a capital letter, a digit or a letter from a script without case, at most 4 words and 40 characters, not already listed), Recap offers to add it to custom vocabulary. With Find in meeting open, Find & replace starts from its words. It is unavailable while a summary, enhanced notes or speaker suggestions are being made for that meeting.
- **Voice profiles** — save a person's voiceprint once and Recap names them automatically in every future meeting (and import) from their first sentence. Three ways to teach it: enroll directly (Settings → Transcription → Voice profiles → "Add a voice…", ~15 s of speech), tap "Save voice" when you name a detected speaker during a meeting, or tag transcript lines after the fact in any meeting that kept its audio — the exact audio behind those lines feeds the profile. Manual tags always override. Voiceprints stay on-device, ride in backups, and are deletable per person.
- **Ask your library** — the sparkle icon on the home screen answers questions across every meeting at once ("what did we decide about pricing?"), with on-device retrieval picking the relevant meetings and answers citing their sources; without an LLM configured you get the matching moments directly, each linking to its meeting. Tapping a source opens the meeting at the moment it quotes first, with the matching word marked by Find in meeting.
- **Quick-record entry points** — long-press the launcher icon for Record / Import shortcuts, add the "Record meeting" Quick Settings tile, or place the **home-screen widget** (Record + Import in a Nocturne card).
- **Translate to English (Whisper)** — a Settings toggle runs Whisper's translate task, so speech in any language is transcribed as English text, fully on-device. Works in live recording, imports, and accuracy checks; needs a multilingual model (English-only models ignore it). The meeting's language chip then reads "Language: German, translated to English" (for German speech), and its summaries are written in English.
- **Topics / auto-chapters, collapsible** — "Topics" on the meeting page splits the transcript into titled chapters: LLM-detected topic shifts when an endpoint is configured, otherwise an offline pass that finds conversational seams and titles sections by their most distinctive keywords. Long meetings are analysed in windows, so chapters cover the whole recording rather than only its opening. Chapters start **collapsed**, turning the transcript into an outline you expand section by section — each header shows its line count and time span, and anything that jumps to a line (playback, search, reminders) expands its section first.
- **Action-item reminders** — long-press any action item → "Remind me…" fires a notification at the chosen time, deep-linking back to the meeting; checking the item off cancels it. Reminders survive reboots.
- **Whiteboard OCR** — attached photos are read on-device (bundled ML Kit Latin model: photos and their text stay on the phone, but ML Kit sends Google device, app and performance diagnostics, as disclosed under Photos and in the privacy policy). Whiteboard text is found by the home search bar and by Ask your library, and feeds LLM summaries automatically.
- **Meeting nudges (optional)** — with "Suggest recording at meeting start" enabled, a notification at each calendar event's start offers one-tap recording; nothing records until you tap.
- **Background import** — file imports run in a foreground service with a progress notification, so you can leave the screen or lock the phone; tap the completion notification to open the meeting. The transcript of a meeting recorded as audio only, and accuracy checks, are made by the same background service. On Android 15 and later it runs as a media-processing foreground service, with a six-hour daily allowance separate from downloads and summaries. Clear-voice copies run as the same type and draw on the same allowance.
- **Interrupted transcriptions carry on** — a transcription stopped part-way now picks up where it stopped instead of starting over.
  - If the charger comes out under "only while charging", the pass goes back in the queue and carries on from the same point the next time the phone charges; the notification says "“Title” will carry on from 42:10 next time you charge".
  - If Android's daily background limit, a crash or force-stop, Cancel or a failure part-way stops it, the words so far are kept and the meeting shows a "This transcript is unfinished. Transcription can carry on from 42:10." card with Continue transcription. The same action is in the meeting's ⋮ menu and in the home card's options ("From 42:10"). After Recap was closed mid-transcription, the "Resume interrupted work?" prompt says where it will carry on, also for an import that had already become a meeting.
  - The point it carries on from is the last pause everything before was finished up to, so lines can run past it (a recording whose background never goes quiet is cut only every 28 s). When there are lines from that point on, Continue first says how many: they are transcribed again and replaced as the transcription reaches them, edits included. If it stops again, the lines it never reached are kept, so the transcript is never left shorter than it was. Continue waits until an accuracy check of the meeting has been reviewed, and a check cannot be applied while the meeting is being transcribed.
  - Continuing uses the model and spoken language that made the words. If that model has been deleted, Recap asks before starting over with the current model, and the new pass's lines replace the existing ones from its first save, edits included. A transcription that stopped before it reached any pause to carry on from can only start over: the card's button then reads Start over, and the home option "From the beginning".
  - Speakers are matched again over the whole recording, so labels agree across the point where it continued, and a name you gave a speaker before that point carries over to their later lines when more than half of their earlier lines have that name. If you summarised the partial transcript, the summary is marked out of date instead of being replaced.
  - Progress reads "Continuing from 42:10 · …", and "matching voices so far" while the earlier voices are re-embedded. MP3 files are decoded from the start up to the resume point, so they take a little longer to begin.
  - An accuracy check stopped by unplugging also carries on from where it stopped the next time the phone charges; one stopped by Cancel, the time limit or an error still starts again from the beginning. Accepting a check clears an unfinished first pass's resume point.
  - The resume point is a number kept on the phone, in the meeting's JSON file (`transcriptResumeMs`, so it is in backups like any other meeting field) and in the local job queue.
- **Follow-ups** — the check icon on the home screen lists every open action item across all meetings; checking one off completes it in its owning meeting (and cancels its reminder), long-press jumps to that meeting.
- **Word-level tap-to-seek** — with the on-device engine and saved audio, every transcript word carries its own timestamp: tap any word on the meeting page to play from that exact moment (Pixel-Recorder style). Timings ride in the meeting JSON and are kept through Edit text, Split and Find & replace (only the changed words are re-timed). With TalkBack on, words are not separate links; use the line's "Play from here" action from the TalkBack actions menu instead.
- **Record device audio (Android 10+)** — long-press the record orb and pick "Device audio" to capture what the phone is playing (webinars, videos, voice notes) via AudioPlaybackCapture, after a one-time screen-capture consent per session. Apps that mark their audio private (most VoIP calls) are excluded by Android; media playback captures fine. Needs the Whisper engine. On Android 15 and later the capture stops if the screen locks or times out, so keep the phone unlocked while recording. Recap warns about this before the consent dialog (with a "Don't show this again" option), and if it happens, the session stops counting and waits for Finish.
- **Meeting audio + synced playback** — with the on-device engine, each meeting keeps a compact on-device recording (about 29 MB per hour: 64 kbps mono AAC in an ADTS stream, playable even if the app dies mid-meeting; toggle under Settings → Transcription → "Keep meeting audio"). A player bar on the meeting page replays it; tap any transcript line → "Play from here" jumps to that exact moment. Share the audio file, or run **Check transcript accuracy…** later (⋮ menu) to have a heavier model listen again and show you where the two passes disagree. Audio is included in backups and deleted with the meeting. ⋮ → Remove audio (keep transcript) deletes just the recording, and automatic clean-up can remove old meetings' audio for you (see Storage and clean-up).
- **Clear voice** — in the meeting's player, tap Clear voice to make a copy of the audio with background noise removed, made on the phone by the bundled GTCRN model through sherpa-onnx. The original recording is not changed. The copy is saved beside the recording as `<recording name without its extension>.clear.m4a` (for example `1234.clear.m4a` beside `1234.m4a`; AAC, 64 kbps, about 29 MB an hour).
  - It asks first, with the copy's approximate size; a meeting of 30 minutes or more also gets its length and a note that plugging in saves battery. It cannot start during a recording.
  - It runs as a foreground job with progress, time left and a Stop button, and keeps going if you leave the screen. Stopping it keeps nothing.
  - Tap the chip again to switch between the copy and the original at the same position. Clips you share come from whichever one is playing.
  - The delete button beside the chip deletes the copy after asking.
  - Transcripts are never made from the copy unless you tick "Transcribe the clear-voice copy instead" in the accuracy check, because denoising can remove parts of words; you review that result before anything changes.
  - Copies count as meeting audio on the Storage screen, each counted with its recording in the largest-recordings list. Remove audio (meeting menu, Storage screen, multi-select, automatic clean-up) and deleting a meeting remove the copy too, and the Remove audio question says so. Backups leave copies out (see Backup & restore).
- **Import recordings, videos and transcripts** — share files to Recap (from the phone's voice recorder, a call platform, a field recorder), open one with Recap from a file manager ("Open with"), or use Import audio on the home screen, whose picker now also takes video and transcripts, several files at once. Everything is read on the phone: the share, Open with and picker grants cover only the files you chose, and Recap asks for no storage permission.
  - **Audio** is decoded and transcribed fully on-device with Whisper, Parakeet or Nemotron, including speaker detection when enabled, and becomes a normal meeting. Cancelling keeps the words so far, and Continue transcription carries on from there (see Interrupted transcriptions).
  - **Video** (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM, 3GP and the like) is imported for its sound only. "Extracting the sound from the video…" copies the audio track out without re-encoding it: AAC into an .m4a, AMR into a .3gp, Vorbis or Opus into an audio-only .webm (Opus from Android 10). A video whose sound cannot be copied out that way (MP3, PCM, AC-3 or FLAC inside the video), or whose copy fails, is stored whole, as before. A file with no sound is refused ("That file has no sound to transcribe.").
  - **Several files at once**, from the share sheet or the picker: each recording becomes its own queued import, transcribed one after another with one model and language choice for all of them. The queue holds at most 20 imports, counting any already waiting; files beyond that, and files that cannot be imported, are listed as left out in a summary. Progress reads "File 2 of 5 · …". Stay on the import screen while files are being added (Back explains why); Cancel stops adding the rest.
  - **Transcripts**: WebVTT (.vtt) and SubRip (.srt) files, such as Zoom's .transcript.vtt and Teams' .vtt, import as meetings without audio. Speakers come from `<v Name>` tags or "Name: text" prefixes ("Speaker 2" becomes voice 2), line times come from the cues, and the meeting's length is the last cue's end. These meetings have no playback, accuracy check, Re-transcribe or voice clustering; summaries, search, editing, speaker names and export work as usual. A transcript is not paired with the recording of the same name: each becomes its own meeting. A file that cannot be read gives a reason, never a crash.
  - Meetings are named from the file name with the extension removed (and Zoom's ".transcript" or ".cc" before ".vtt"), underscores read as spaces.
- **Capture tuning (Whisper engine)** — pick how the phone pre-processes the mic ("Far-field" camcorder tuning often beats the default for a phone on the table; "Raw" disables processing where supported) and choose the input device, including USB and wired external microphones.
- **Highlights** — a star button while recording marks the current moment — or tap Mark moment on the recording notification, from the shade or the lock screen, without unlocking (it also appears when the lock screen hides notification content, and names nothing about the meeting there). The notification briefly reads "Marked at 0:12:34"; it only adds stars, never removes one, and is hidden while paused and once Finish is tapped. In an audio-only recording the mark stars the line the after-meeting transcript gives that moment; if that transcription stops part-way, a mark later than its last line now waits until the transcript reaches it instead of starring whatever line was last at the time. Marks are also no longer dropped when the meeting is saved from the meeting screen. Long-press any transcript line afterwards to toggle. Highlighted moments get priority treatment in summaries. With TalkBack, the long-press is announced as "Highlight" / "Remove highlight", and each line's actions menu also offers Edit text, Split and Fix a word everywhere, plus Play from here and Share clip when the meeting kept its audio.
- **Ask this meeting** — with an LLM endpoint configured, ask free-form questions on the meeting page ("what did we decide about pricing?"); answers come strictly from the transcript, notes, and summary, and the Q&A thread is saved with the meeting.
- **Summary templates** — choose the summary's shape when generating: **Executive Summary**, **Note to Self** (a private reminder of what *you* committed to — set your name in Settings so it can tell), General minutes, Action items, Decisions & open questions, Standup, Sales call report, 1:1, Job interview, User research, Retrospective, Project status, Follow-up email, plus domain shapes — **Clinical visit note (SOAP)**, **Patient consult recap**, **Legal consultation memo**, and **Case/matter notes** (all framed as drafts for professional review, with a fixed notice; see below) — and your own **custom templates** (name + free-form instructions, saved on-device, managed from the picker's "Custom…" button). A **Brief / Standard / Detailed** toggle in the picker sets how deep any template goes, recurring meetings **remember their template per series** (your standup preselects Standup, your sales calls preselect the call report), and every template — custom ones included — now runs with shared output rules: verbatim names/dates/amounts, no invented facts, no filler sections, no preamble.
- **Fixed notices on clinical and legal summaries** — summaries made with the clinical templates (SOAP note, patient recap) or the legal templates (legal consultation memo, case/matter notes), and any summary written by MedGemma, carry a fixed notice that is not AI-generated: "AI-generated draft for review by a qualified clinician … not a medical record, a diagnosis or medical advice", or "… by a qualified lawyer … not legal advice". It appears above the summary and in every share, copy and Markdown/PDF export, the pre-meeting brief, the weekly digest and Ask your library answers built from that summary.
- **Report AI output** — on the meeting summary, each Ask this meeting answer, the meeting's ⋮ menu, Ask your library, the weekly digest (written, and offline when it quotes a model-written summary), the pre-meeting brief (its generated brief and its Last time card), the live Catch me up sheet and Follow-ups. Extractive text (key points worked out on the spot, or a summary recorded as extractive) is not offered for report. It opens a dialog with a reason and an optional comment, then an email draft to the developer's support address in your own email app (or the clipboard, if no email app is installed). The generated text is included only if you tick the box, which is off by default. Nothing is sent unless you send the email.
- **Structured action items** — summaries also produce a checkable to-do list with owners (from your speaker tags or the LLM); check items off, long-press to remove.
- **Photo attachments** — snap the whiteboard from the recording screen or add camera/gallery photos on the meeting page; stored privately with the meeting.
- **Export** — the meeting's Export dialog offers Markdown, PDF, Word document (.docx), Subtitles (.srt), Subtitles (.vtt), action items to .ics and action items as text. Files go to a place you choose in the system file picker (action items as text go to the share sheet). Markdown and PDF hold the summary, action items, notes and the timestamped speaker transcript.
  - **Subtitles** are built from lines that have a time in the recording. Lines typed by hand or from Android's speech service are left out, and the toast says how many; a meeting with no timed lines at all shows one "Subtitles (not available for this meeting)" entry that explains why. Long lines are cut at about 7 s or 84 characters (two rows of 42). The speaker appears as "Name: " in SRT and as a `<v Name>` voice tag in VTT. Times are relative to the meeting's recording, and still are after Remove audio.
  - **Word** holds the title, date, duration (estimated from the end of the last transcript line), attendees, tags, the summary with its fixed clinical or legal notice where one applies, action items, notes, and the transcript with times and speakers. No author is written into the document properties. Photos and attachments are not embedded; a line says how many photos the meeting has.
  - **Export library as Markdown** — Settings → Data. Pick a folder, and Recap writes one "yyyy-MM-dd Title.md" per meeting. Each starts with YAML front matter (title, date as ISO 8601 with time-zone offset, duration, attendees, tags, language, `translated_from` when the transcript was translated, id), then the same Markdown as the meeting's own export, so the files work as notes in Obsidian and other Markdown notes apps. Audio, photos and attachments are not included. Meetings being recorded, transcribed or summarised are skipped and counted. Existing files are never overwritten: a name already in the folder gets " (2)" and so on. Progress and Stop show in Settings, files written before a Stop stay, and the final count shows there or, if Settings is closed, as a toast. It runs without a notification, so Android may pause it while Recap is in the background. On Android 11 and later the folder picker will not allow the root of internal storage or Download; create or pick a subfolder. Recap holds access to the folder only while the export runs.
- **Auto-titling** — meetings left with the default title get named from their content after recording (instant on-device heuristic, refined by the LLM when configured).
- **On-device AI (embedded)** — Settings → Summaries → engine "On-device" runs an LLM (llama.cpp v1.0.0, GGUF) entirely on the phone: summaries, Ask, digest, topics, and speaker suggestions with zero network involvement once the model is downloaded. Fifteen models from 0.5 GB to 5.7 GB, with the estimated smallest phone each runs on: Qwen 2.5 0.5B (2 GB of RAM), Gemma 3 1B (3 GB), Qwen 2.5 1.5B (4 GB; the recommended default), Llama 3.2 3B and Gemma 3 4B (6 GB), Gemma 4 E2B (8 GB), Gemma 4 E4B, Mistral 7B and Llama 3.1 8B (12 GB) and Qwen 3.5 9B (16 GB); plus a domain specialist — **MedGemma 4B** (Google's medical Gemma tune, strong on clinical vocabulary; 6 GB). Labels state the requirement, and the picker's fit badges carry the same figures as estimates. Domain summaries (clinical and legal templates alike) are drafts, not clinical or legal documents. Reasoning models' think-aloud blocks are stripped automatically. The attention cache runs quantized (q8_0 + flash attention, with automatic fp16 fallback), freeing ~0.5 GB on the 7-9B tier so big models survive longer on 12 GB phones. Slower than a server; long meetings are handled with a map-reduce pass — the transcript is condensed section-by-section on-device, then summarized from the ordered notes, so hour-long meetings get full coverage (at the cost of a few extra minutes of compute). Quality sits below big cloud models, privacy sits above everything.
  - **Opt-in models** — four entries are offered but never suggested automatically, because none has yet been checked on a phone for summary quality or speed: **Gemma 4 E2B QAT** and **Gemma 4 E4B QAT** (Google's own quantisation-aware-trained Q4_0 files, about 3.35 GB and 5.15 GB, 8 GB+ and 12 GB+ RAM), offered next to the existing Gemma 4 files, and **Qwen 3.5 2B** and **Qwen 3.5 4B** (bartowski's Q4_K_M conversions, about 1.40 GB and 3.01 GB, 4 GB+ and 8 GB+ RAM). The suggested default stays Qwen 2.5 1.5B.
  - **Licences** — each model's licence is shown on its card in the model picker and in Settings → Open-source licences. Qwen 2.5 0.5B/1.5B, Qwen 3.5 2B/4B/9B and Gemma 4 (the unsloth files and Google's QAT builds alike) are Apache 2.0 — Gemma 4's from Google's Gemma 4 licence page — and ask for no acceptance; Mistral 7B is labelled "see model page". Only the first download of a Gemma 3, MedGemma or Llama model asks you once to accept its licence (Gemma Terms of Use, Health AI Developer Foundations Terms of Use, Llama 3.2/3.1 Community License). MedGemma is governed by the Health AI Developer Foundations Terms of Use, not the Gemma Terms. Llama 3.2 3B and Llama 3.1 8B are Built with Llama.
  - **Longer window for small models** — the small models (Qwen 2.5 0.5B and 1.5B, Gemma 3 1B and Qwen 3.5 2B) read 8,192 tokens at once instead of 4,096 on phones with room for it, so they summarise a meeting of up to roughly 30-35 minutes in a single pass instead of about 15, and a longer meeting takes about half as many condensing passes, so its summary finishes sooner. 3B and larger models keep 4,096, and so does any phone that cannot hold the larger window comfortably (by DeviceFit's estimates the larger window needs a 3 GB phone for Qwen 2.5 0.5B, 4 GB for Gemma 3 1B and 6 GB for Qwen 2.5 1.5B and Qwen 3.5 2B). The larger window is granted only where the model still fits comfortably with it, so the fit badges and first-run suggestions are unchanged.
  - **Leaner Gemma cache on tight phones** — where a Gemma model (Gemma 3, MedGemma or Gemma 4, which use sliding-window attention) is tight on this phone's memory (its badge is not "Fits this phone"), the engine keeps a smaller cache for those sliding-window layers. Answers are the same and memory use is lower; a follow-up Ask question may re-read the meeting instead of reusing the previous prompt, which is slower but never wrong.
  - **Meeting text cannot forge chat-control tokens** — on-device, text from a meeting (transcript, imported file, text read from photos, notes, titles, attendee names, your questions) is tokenised as plain text, so it can no longer act as the model's chat-control tokens (`<|im_end|>`, `<end_of_turn>`, `<|eot_id|>`, `<|turn>` and the like), and the internal message separators (U+001E/U+001F) inside it can no longer start a forged message. This hardens on-device summaries and Ask against prompt injection from meeting content (it does not stop plain-language instructions written into a meeting, which the model may still follow). It does not apply to a remote AI endpoint, which builds its own prompts from the text it receives.
  - **Arm KleidiAI** — both arm64 builds of the native library include Arm's KleidiAI v1.32.0 kernels, which ggml picks at run time from the processor's features, so there is no new CPU tier and no new risk of crashing on an older processor. On a phone with dot-product instructions they take the Q6_K weights of the Q4_K_M models (requantised to 8-bit, which holds those tensors in about 1.2 times their file size) and all of the Q4_0 weights of the Gemma 4 QAT files; the Q4_K bulk of the other models stays on ggml's own kernels. Cortex-A53/A73-class phones, which lack dot product, run exactly as before, and Whisper models stay on ggml's own kernels everywhere. The speed and memory effect has not been measured on a phone.
- **Summaries**, two ways:
  - **On-device (default, zero config)**: an offline extractive summarizer produces key points and detected action items. No network involved.
  - **LLM (optional)**: point the app at any OpenAI-compatible endpoint — Ollama running on your computer (`http://YOUR_PC_IP:11434/v1`), Groq, OpenRouter, etc. Configure URL, model, and API key in Settings. If the LLM call fails, the app falls back to the on-device summary.
- **Background summarization with live progress** — generating a summary runs in a foreground service, so it survives rotation, navigation, and the screen turning off. Progress is visible everywhere: a banner on the home screen (tap to open the meeting), a real progress bar in the notification shade (map-reduce sections count up; "writing the summary" near the end), and a "Summary ready" notification when it finishes.
- **Enhance notes** — Granola-style: type rough bullets during the meeting, then tap Enhance and the AI expands them into complete notes using the transcript for the specifics you didn't have time to type. Your structure and voice stay; a Revert button restores the original at any time. Runs in the same background service as summaries, with the same progress banner/notification.
- **Catch me up** — mid-meeting, tap the sparkle button on the recording screen for a terse "what happened so far" (current topic, decisions, open questions). Uses your configured AI when available, the instant offline summarizer otherwise.
- **Playback speed + skip silence** — 0.5×–3× playback, and a skip-silence toggle that jumps over non-speech stretches (derived from the transcript's own timings — no audio analysis, works on any saved meeting with Whisper word data).
- **Rename any meeting** — ⋮ → Rename on the meeting page.
- **Long meetings open without freezing** — the meeting page shows the title and date at once (handed over by the list, or read from the start of the file when it is opened from a notification) and reads the rest of the meeting off the main thread, on the same worker the screen saves on, so the read always comes after a save the previous screen queued. If the read takes more than about a quarter of a second, an "Opening meeting…" progress row appears under the date, and TalkBack announces it once. The toolbar and ⋮ actions stay disabled until the meeting is read. Rotating the meeting page now keeps the selected tab (Summary, Transcript or Audio), the collapsed chapters and the scroll position, where it used to go back to the Summary tab, and the "follows on from" offer after a recording survives a rotation until you answer or dismiss it.
- **Storage and clean-up** — Settings → Data → Storage and clean-up opens the Storage screen, and so does Android's App info → Storage → Manage space for Recap (which therefore no longer offers Android's own clear-storage dialog). After "Measuring storage…" it shows:
  - what each area takes: meeting audio (clear-voice copies included); photos and attachments; transcripts and meeting data; Whisper models; Parakeet and Nemotron models; speaker ID models; on-device AI models; caches (clips, waveforms, shared files); voice profiles and other data. The total leaves out the installed app itself. Free space is shown beside it.
  - the five largest recordings (each counted with its clear-voice copy), each of which can be opened or have its audio removed;
  - **Delete unused models**: downloaded models that are not selected for transcription, speaker ID or on-device AI, not waiting for or in a download, not named by a queued accuracy check or import, and not the model the automatic accuracy check would use. While a recording, import, summary or model download runs it deletes nothing and asks you to try again once that has finished; any model can be downloaded again from Settings.
  - **Remove audio (keep transcript)**, also in the meeting's ⋮ menu and in multi-select, deletes the recording, its waveform cache and any shared clips cut from it, and keeps the transcript, summary, notes, photos and attachments. Afterwards playback and follow-along, sharing the audio or a clip, accuracy checks, learning a voice from the recording and re-transcription no longer work, and the confirmation lists them. It is refused while the meeting is being recorded, transcribed or summarised, or has a queued accuracy check or import or an accuracy check waiting for review. A meeting with no transcript yet gets a sterner question on the meeting page and the Storage screen ("Delete this meeting's only recording?"), and multi-select and automatic clean-up leave such meetings out. It also deletes the meeting's clear-voice copy, and the question says so. Multi-select and automatic clean-up also leave out meetings whose transcription is unfinished; removing the audio of one from its menu or the Storage screen asks first and says the transcription can then never be completed, and afterwards Continue transcription disappears and the meeting keeps the words it has.
  - **Automatic clean-up**, off by default, set here or in Settings → Data: remove the audio of, or delete, meetings older than 30, 60, 90, 180 or 365 days. Choosing a rule first shows what it would reach now and saves it only if you confirm. After that a daily background job (when the battery is not low) applies it and posts a quiet "Storage clean-up" notification only when it removed something; turning the rule off cancels the job. Never touched: starred meetings, meetings changed in the last 7 days, meetings with a reminder still to come, the meeting being recorded, meetings open on a meeting screen, meetings with a running, queued or interrupted job or a staged accuracy check or speaker suggestions waiting for review, and meetings with no date. Removing old audio also skips recordings that have no transcript yet, or whose transcription is unfinished. If a daily run would delete more than 10 whole meetings, it deletes nothing and posts a "due for clean-up" notification that opens a review instead. All of it happens on the phone; nothing is sent.
- **Meeting language**
  - Settings → Transcription → Spoken language: Auto-detect (the default) or one of 36 languages. Multilingual Whisper models are told the language instead of detecting it, and Nemotron 3.5 gets it as its language prompt (for every listed language except Indonesian and Malay, which it has no setting for and which stay on auto there). Parakeet v3 and Ultra take no hint and always detect the language themselves (the meeting still records the pinned language, which summaries then follow), and English-only models always transcribe English. A wrong choice turns the transcript into nonsense, which is why Auto stays the default.
  - Import: when a downloaded model can take a language, the model sheet has a Spoken language row for that one file, defaulting to the Settings value. The sheet appears for this even when only one model is downloaded, if that model can take a language; a single model that cannot (Parakeet, or an English-only model) starts straight away.
  - The meeting page shows a "Language: German" chip ("not recorded" on older meetings that kept their audio, and ", translated to English" when Whisper's translate option made the transcript; hidden when the language is unknown and there is no audio). On Auto-detect only multilingual Whisper records the language it heard; a Parakeet or Nemotron 3.5 run on auto leaves it "not recorded". Tapping the chip opens Re-transcribe as…: a language (or Auto-detect, which needs a multilingual Whisper model), a model that can take it, and a confirmation with the time estimate. It runs as an accuracy check: the new transcript is shown beside the old one and nothing is replaced until you accept it, and accepting the whole transcript stores its language. On a meeting with no transcript yet, the result becomes its transcript.
  - Settings → Summaries → Summary language: Same as meeting (the default) or a fixed language. It applies to AI summaries, Ask this meeting answers, enhanced notes and titles, with an endpoint and with the on-device engine. A transcript translated to English is summarised in English. Small on-device models can slip back into English. With Same as meeting, English meetings and meetings whose language was never recorded get the same prompts as before.
  - The offline summariser and the offline action-item fallback pick out action items from English transcripts only. For a meeting known to be in another language, the offline summary says so instead of listing false matches, and the fallback adds none.
- **Transcript text size** — Small / Medium / Large in Settings → Appearance, on top of your system font size.
- **Accessibility** — 48dp touch targets throughout; TalkBack names every gesture-only action (transcript lines, action items, meeting cards, photos, files); chapter headers are headings with expanded/collapsed state; the Audio tab's speaker strip offers next/previous speaker turn; recording state changes and import, summary and model-download progress are announced at milestones, never per tick; a meeting that takes a moment to open is announced once ("Opening meeting…").
- **Custom vocabulary** — list names, acronyms, and jargon in Settings and Whisper is biased toward them (its initial prompt) for all new recordings and imports. One-tap **medical and legal starter glossaries** seed the list with commonly fumbled domain terms (drug names, procedural Latin…) — prune and add your own; your names and jargon matter most. Only Whisper models use the list (Parakeet and Nemotron ignore it), and Whisper sees only about its first 600 characters, so put the terms that matter most first. Find & replace offers to add a name or term it corrected. It goes at the start of the list so Whisper always sees it. On a long list this can push your last terms past the ~600-character limit. Adding it also saves the list comma-separated and drops repeated terms.
- **Guided voice enrollment** — saving a voice profile now shows a short read-aloud script sized to the 15-second capture window, so voiceprints come from continuous, phonetically varied speech instead of improvised filler.
- **Nine Whisper models, per-import choice** — alongside tiny/base/small, the manager now offers quantized Small Q variants (~181 MB, small-quality at less than half the size) and Large v3 Turbo Q (~547 MB, near-flagship accuracy). When importing a file or running an accuracy check, a picker asks which downloaded model to use for that run — grab Turbo for important recordings, keep a fast model as the live-capture default.
- **Device fit and free space** — every model card in the pickers (transcription, speaker and on-device AI models, and the per-import picker) shows an estimated memory fit for this phone: Fits this phone, Tight fit or Too big, with "slow on this processor" added on phones without ARMv8.2 dot-product cores. The figures come from the phone's RAM and an estimate of each model's working set, so choosing a tight or too-big model asks first ("Use anyway") but is never refused. An on-device LLM is costed at the window it will really get on this phone; since a small model's larger 8K window is granted only where it still fits comfortably, its badge is the same as at 4K. Before any model download the app checks free space (the model's size plus 10%, counting downloads already queued) and, if short, offers Android's storage manager or "Download anyway".
- **Download queue** — model downloads run in the background (foreground service + wakelock) and further picks queue up: tap a transcription model, a speaker model, and an on-device LLM back-to-back and they download consecutively under one notification (with a per-model summary at the end). Cancel stops the current download and clears the queue.
- **NVIDIA Parakeet & Nemotron engines** — the model manager also offers Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 (English) and v3 (25 languages), plus Nemotron Streaming 0.6B (English) and Nemotron 3.5 Streaming 0.6B (40 locales), running fully on-device through the bundled sherpa-onnx runtime (v2.0.3 with ONNX Runtime 1.28.2; ~630 MB int8 downloads each). They slot into the same recording/import pipeline as Whisper — diarization, word tap-to-seek, and the per-import picker all work — with top-tier accuracy at strong speed. Translate-to-English and custom vocabulary are Whisper-only and don't apply to these engines. Of these engines only Nemotron 3.5 uses the Spoken language setting, as its language prompt; Parakeet takes no language hint and always detects the language itself. Offered next to v3, opt-in, is **Parakeet Ultra 0.6B** (25 languages, Moondream): Moondream's further training of v3, with the same graph and the same 25 languages, CC BY 4.0 (credited to Moondream and NVIDIA), about 640 MB. It is never suggested automatically, and the accuracy-check picker ranks it with v3, not above it: its published gains come from benchmark sets, and nobody has compared the two on meetings recorded on a phone. The int8 conversion currently on Hugging Face (commit 402e1190, last changed 2026-11-07; checked again for Batch 5, with no newer export) predates sherpa-onnx #4179 (merged 2026-11-12), which keeps some of the encoder in float32 and recovers about two WER points of the int8 loss in English; the revision is not pinned, so a re-upload under the same file names would reach new downloads, while existing installs keep the files they have.
- **Conversation insights** — per-speaker talk-time share with bars, turn counts, longest monologue, questions asked, and speaking pace on every meeting page (estimates derived from the transcript).
- **Pre-meeting brief** — before a recurring meeting, the calendar nudge gains a "Prep brief" action: last time's outcomes, open action items across the series (checkable), and an optional AI-written brief. Also reachable from any meeting's menu.
- **Share audio clips** — long-press a transcript line → "Share clip…" cuts that moment (±1 s) into a small WAV for the share sheet, with the quote attached as text.
- **Send follow-ups to your task app** — export open action items (from one meeting or the whole library) as a .ics tasks file or share them as a text checklist to any app. Reminders become DUE dates.
- **Local storage** — meetings are JSON files in the app's private storage. Meeting content leaves the phone only if you share or export it, switch to Android's speech service, or turn on an AI endpoint. Other network use carries no meeting content: model downloads you ask for, and ML Kit's diagnostics to Google (see Whiteboard OCR). A summarised meeting's JSON also records `summaryTemplate` (the template key) and `summaryModel` (`local:<model key>`, `endpoint:<model name>` or `extractive`); both are left out when unknown and never contain the endpoint address or API key. From 3.18.0-beta1 it also records `language` (the spoken language code, detected or pinned) and `translated` (true when Whisper's translate option made the transcript); each is left out when unknown or false. From 3.19.0-beta1 it can also record `transcriptResumeMs` (where an unfinished transcription stopped, in milliseconds of the recording; left out once the transcript is complete) and `clearVoiceFile` (the clear-voice copy's file name, only once the copy is complete).
- **Share/export** — share a meeting (summary + notes + transcript) as text to any app.
- **Local crash reports** — if Recap crashes, a report is saved on the phone and offered on the next launch through your own share sheet (Settings → Report a bug does the same on demand); nothing is sent unless you share it. On Android 11 and later Recap also reads Android's own record of how it last closed (ApplicationExitInfo), so native crashes in the whisper/llama/sherpa engines, freezes (ANRs) and memory kills become the same kind of local report ("Recap stopped unexpectedly last time"), noting which kind of on-device work (transcription, speech detection, summary, speaker detection, photo text) and which model were running. A native crash's report carries a decoded backtrace (Android 12+); Android's full crash record stays beside it on the phone and is not part of the shared text.

## Getting the APK

Every push touching `android/` builds an APK via GitHub Actions (`Build Android APK` workflow). Download the `meetily-android-apk` artifact from the workflow run, unzip it, and install `app-release.apk` on your phone (you'll need to allow "install unknown apps" for your browser/file manager).

When the repository's `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_*` secrets are set, the APK is signed with the upload key, so every build carries the same certificate and installs over the previous one. Without them it falls back to a throwaway debug key that changes on every run, so each build has to be installed fresh (see PLAY_RELEASE.md §1). As Android developer verification rolls out (globally in 2027), APKs installed outside Play on certified devices need their signing key registered, so the upload key has to be registered for `com.recap.mobile` in Play Console (see the owner checklist in PLAY_RELEASE.md).

## Building locally

Requirements: JDK 17+, Android SDK Platform 36 (compileSdk/targetSdk 36) and Build-Tools 35.0.0, NDK 27 + CMake 3.22 (installed automatically by the Android Gradle Plugin; the pinned NDK is 27.0.12077973 and CMake is 3.22.1). The build uses AGP 8.10.1 with the Gradle 8.14.3 wrapper and Kotlin 2.0.21. The native module fetches llama.cpp v1.0.0 and whisper.cpp b5907 (pinned release tarballs; both embed the released ggml 1.0.0, and whisper.cpp has no release tag on it yet, hence the build tag) at CMake configure time, and on arm64 ggml also downloads Arm KleidiAI v1.32.0 (Apache-2.0; URL and MD5 pinned by ggml) for both the `:app` and `:native-v82` builds, so the first build needs network access to github.com. CI keeps an MD5-checked KleidiAI copy in `actions/cache` and passes it in with the `kleidiaiSourceDir` Gradle property; locally, `-PkleidiaiSourceDir=<extracted kleidiai-v1.32.0 directory>` does the same (ggml's MD5 check is then skipped, so check the tarball as the workflows do).

whisper.cpp, llama.cpp, llama-common, the Silero speech gate and one ggml (with KleidiAI on arm64) are linked, with one static libc++, into one native library per CPU level: `libmeetily_native.so` (plain armv8-a on arm64, and x86_64) and `libmeetily_native_v82.so` (armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16, arm64 only, built by `:native-v82`). Until Batch 5 these were two libraries per level, `libmeetily_whisper` and `libmeetily_llama`, each with its own ggml and libc++; the compile flags per level are unchanged. `NativeEngines` loads the `_v82` build when every core has dot-product and half-precision instructions, and falls back to the baseline if it does not load.

sherpa-onnx (Parakeet, Nemotron, speaker models and the Clear voice denoiser) is not built here: `scripts/fetch_sherpa_libs.sh` downloads the v2.0.3 Android release (which bundles ONNX Runtime 1.28.2; it was v1.13.4 with 1.27.0 before Batch 5), checks the tarball against the SHA-256 pinned in the script, and installs `libsherpa-onnx-jni.so` and `libonnxruntime.so` for arm64-v8a and x86_64 into the gitignored `app/src/main/jniLibs`. CI then runs `scripts/check_sherpa_jni.py`, which checks that every native the vendored Kotlin API declares is exported on both ABIs, that both libraries are 16 KB aligned, that they need nothing beyond the system libraries and each other, and that the version stamp matches the pin. The move to v2.0.3 grows arm64 native code by about 1.2 MB uncompressed (`libsherpa-onnx-jni.so` 4,710,728 → 5,386,920 bytes, `libonnxruntime.so` 21,688,912 → 22,249,560); on x86_64 the pair is 5,913,864 + 25,581,120 bytes. See ANDROID_LOCAL_DEV.md for the local steps.

```bash
cd android
bash scripts/fetch_sherpa_libs.sh && python3 scripts/check_sherpa_jni.py   # sherpa-onnx prebuilts, checked
./gradlew testReleaseUnitTest   # pure-logic unit tests
./gradlew assembleRelease       # sideload APK (upload key if configured, else debug key)
python3 scripts/check_jni_exports.py app/build/outputs/apk/release/*.apk   # the native library exports only its Java_ entry points
```

The native library (both CPU builds) is linked with a version script (`app/src/main/cpp/jni_exports.map`) that exports only its `Java_` entry points. CI checks the built APK/AAB against `app/src/main/cpp/jni_exports.txt`, which NativeExportsTest keeps in step with the Kotlin natives. Adding or renaming a native means updating that list; the test prints the corrected file.

## Releasing (signed, Play-ready)

Without a keystore, the sideload APK from `assembleRelease` is signed with the debug key. For a real release, provide an upload keystore — the build picks it up automatically:

- **Locally:** create `android/keystore.properties` with `storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias`, `keyPassword` (this file is gitignored), then run `./gradlew bundleRelease` for a signed `.aab`.
- **CI:** the `Release Android (signed AAB)` workflow (manual dispatch) builds a signed AAB + APK from repository secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS`, `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`.

When no keystore is configured the release build falls back to the debug key, so day-to-day CI stays green without secrets.

## Deploying to the Play Store — checklist

The full runbook, including the owner checklist to complete before submission, is in [PLAY_RELEASE.md](PLAY_RELEASE.md). In short:

- Provide an upload keystore (above) and enable Play App Signing.
- Declare the foreground-service types in Play Console: microphone, mediaProjection, dataSync and, new in 3.16.0-beta1, mediaProcessing (ImportService on Android 15+). From 3.19.0-beta1 ClearVoiceService uses the same two types as ImportService (mediaProcessing on Android 15+, dataSync below) to make a clear-voice copy the user asked for, so update both descriptions; no new type or permission. PLAY_RELEASE.md §5 has the wording and the demo videos to record.

## Notes and limitations

- With the opt-in Android speech service engine, speech recognition uses the device's recognition service (Google's on most phones). Continuous listening is implemented by restarting the recognizer between utterances, so an occasional word can be missed at segment boundaries. The recognizer's start/stop chime is muted during recording by default (Settings → "Mute recognizer chime while recording"); turn that off if you need phone audio (e.g. a call on speaker) to stay audible while recording.
- On-device (offline) recognition requires Android 12+ and the language pack downloaded in system settings; otherwise the device's default (usually networked) recognizer is used.
- Meeting audio is kept only with the on-device engine (toggleable); the system-engine path stores transcription only.
- Capturing the other side of a phone/VoIP call is restricted by Android for third-party apps; this app transcribes what the microphone hears (in-person meetings, or calls on speaker).
- Diagnostics in logcat, for support (shape only, never prompt, reply or transcript text): `meetily_llama` logs each model load (`local model loaded (n_ctx=..., requested ..., trained ...; n_swa=..., full/small/no SWA cache)`), each prompt's size (`prompt: ... jinja=1, spans=..., guarded=...`), an info line when a message spelled a control token, and `reusing N cached prompt tokens of M` when a prompt starts like the previous one (an Ask follow-up, for example). `meetily_ggml` carries llama.cpp's warnings and errors and its load-time lines: `load_tensors: CPU_KLEIDIAI model buffer size = ... MiB` (or `CPU_REPACK`/`CPU`), the KV and compute buffer sizes, the KV cache's cell counts and KleidiAI's kernel choice (`kleidiai: primary q8 kernel feature DOTPROD` or `I8MM`, `kleidiai: SME disabled`). Since the engines share one library and one ggml, it also carries whisper.cpp's own warnings and errors (model file problems, a prompt trimmed to fit, a language code), and KleidiAI's kernel-choice line appears once per process, at whichever engine's first model or speech-gate load comes first, so it may come with a Whisper or speech-gate load rather than the LLM's. `meetily_native` logs `loaded libmeetily_native_v82.so` (or `libmeetily_native.so`) once per process, `libmeetily_native_v82.so did not load: ...` when the fast build is skipped, and `native engines unavailable: ...` once if neither loads. `meetily_whisper` logs `whisper_full: N encoder pass(es)`, which reads 2 on a call that auto-detects the language (the first of a file: that pass always existed, and whisper.cpp b5907 now reports it), and `VAD model loaded`; `meetily_vad` logs the speech detector's one "speech gate off" warning if it cannot run. Stop also takes effect during Whisper's language detection now. `SherpaEmbedder` logs `speaker model <key> rejected: ...` when a speaker model fails the structure check, and `meetily_clearvoice` one `clear voice: ...` warning per kind of problem when a clear-voice copy cannot be made (the bundled model's copy, load or run, or the encoder). To see them all: `adb logcat -s meetily_native meetily_ggml meetily_llama meetily_whisper meetily_vad meetily_clearvoice SherpaEmbedder`.
- Cleartext traffic is configured by `res/xml/network_security_config.xml`, not `usesCleartextTraffic`. Cleartext is allowed by default only so private-network AI endpoints (e.g. Ollama on your LAN) work, and EndpointGuard enforces that in code by refusing plain http to any public address; model downloads are https-only in code (every redirect included) and in the config.
