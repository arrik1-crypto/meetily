# Meetily for Android

A lightweight, privacy-first companion app to the Meetily desktop app: record meetings on your phone, get a transcript made on the phone (live as you speak, if you turn that on), take notes alongside it, and generate a summary — all stored locally on the device.

This is a standalone native Android app (Kotlin). It does **not** depend on the desktop Rust core or the archived Python backend.

## Features

- **Transcription on the phone**, with two engines:
  - **On-device** (default): Whisper, Parakeet or Nemotron running on the phone (whisper.cpp and sherpa-onnx are compiled into the app, arm64/x86_64). A new install offers a model suited to the phone on its first run (Set up on-device AI); otherwise download one in Settings → Transcription (Whisper Tiny 75 MB up to Large v3 Turbo Q 547 MB, or a ~630 MB Parakeet/Nemotron model); until then meetings are recorded as audio only and can be transcribed afterwards from the meeting's ⋮ → Check transcript accuracy. By default the transcript is made when you stop, which is more accurate and, while recording, uses about a tenth of the battery that live transcription does; turn on Settings → "Transcribe live" to see words as they are spoken. With it on, the microphone opens at once and the model loads while the recording runs, so the first words are kept. If the live model fails to load, the meeting carries on as an audio recording, the recording screen says which model did not load, and the transcript is made after the meeting ends. If Finish comes while the model is still loading, Recap waits for the load to end before saving (a minute after Finish at most, with "Finishing transcription…" showing). The meeting then gets its words from the live model or, if the load fails, from its audio after the meeting ends, and its audio is not transcribed beside a model still loading. With "Keep meeting audio" off, the audio captured while the model loads is held (up to 90 s) and kept only if the load fails, so that transcript starts with the meeting. After Finish the live model is freed once its current chunk is done, and words still waiting 15 s after Finish with "Keep meeting audio" off are added afterwards. See Notes and limitations.
  - **Android speech service** (opt-in): instant partial results, but Android documents it as likely to stream audio to remote servers (Google's on most phones), it keeps no audio, and switching to it asks for confirmation. On Android 12+ you can prefer the phone's offline recognizer (Settings → "Prefer on-device transcription").
  - Installs upgraded from earlier versions keep the system engine and are offered the on-device one once.
- **Speech detection before transcription** — after the existing loudness check, a small Silero VAD model scores each chunk of audio on the phone, and a chunk with no speech in it is never sent to Whisper, Parakeet or Nemotron. That means fewer recogniser passes over silence, steady fan or air-conditioning noise and typing, and less text invented from noise. It applies wherever the on-device engine transcribes: live transcription, the transcript made after a recording, imports, and accuracy checks. A chunk that is kept reaches the recogniser unchanged, so its timestamps, tap-to-seek positions and speaker labels are the same as without the detector. Settings → Transcription (on-device section) → "Skip silence with speech detection" is on by default; turning it off restores the earlier loudness-only behaviour, and is worth trying if a very quiet speaker is being missed. It is not a music filter (music can score as speech, and is then transcribed as before), and it is not always faster: the detector itself costs roughly a minute per hour of audio on a fast core, so the saving depends on how much of a recording is loud but not speech. Silero VAD v6.2 (MIT, Silero Team, in the ggml form the whisper.cpp project publishes) is built into the app (`ggml-silero-v6.2.0.bin`, 885,098 bytes, about 0.9 MB). The only other bundled models are the GTCRN speech enhancer behind Clear voice (`gtcrn_simple.onnx`, 535,638 bytes) and ML Kit's Latin text recogniser for photos; every other speech or language model is still downloaded on request, through the licence and free-space checks. On first use it is copied once into the app's private no-backup storage; it runs entirely on the phone, sends nothing and needs no network. If the copy, the library, the model or its built-in self-test fails, Recap transcribes every chunk as before and writes one "speech gate off" warning to logcat (tag `meetily_vad`). This is separate from the playback "skip silence" toggle, which works from the transcript's timings. The detector runs through whisper.cpp, in the same native library as the engines, so a Parakeet or Nemotron run loads that library for it. sherpa-onnx's own Silero VAD was looked at again after the move to sherpa-onnx v2.0.3, which fixed its long-stream crash (#4079) and its Flush sample drop (#4147), and was not adopted: it would need a second, different Silero export bundled, re-tuned thresholds and a new self-test, for no benefit found (the reasoning is in ACCELERATION_FINDINGS.md).
- **Global search** across all meetings — titles, transcripts, notes, summaries, attendees, tags, action items and their owners, text read from attached photos (whiteboards, slides), and attached file names — from the search bar on the main screen. Under each matching meeting the list shows the words around the match, with the match highlighted: after its time into the recording ("0:42:10 · …") for a transcript line, or labelled Notes, Summary, Other summary (a summary kept from another style; see Several summaries per meeting), Action item or Photo text when only one of those matched. A match only in the title, tags, attendees or a file name shows no snippet. Tapping a meeting found by a transcript line opens it on the Transcript tab at that line, with its chapter expanded and the line outlined briefly; when the line's own words matched, Find in meeting opens already searching for what you typed. With TalkBack a row reads "Match at 0:42:10: …", and opening a hit moves focus to the line it landed on.
- **Find in meeting** — the search icon in the meeting page's toolbar opens a find bar over the transcript: every match is marked, "3 of 12" counts them, and previous / next step through them, wrapping round at either end and expanding collapsed chapters on the way. It ignores case, and accents in Latin, Greek and Cyrillic text ("cafe" finds "Café"). Back or the close button closes it and clears the marks. It only finds; Find & replace is a separate dialog.
- **Calendar integration** — start recording during a scheduled event and the meeting's title and guest list pre-fill from your calendar (read-only, toggleable, permission asked on first use); a "From calendar" button lets you pick the event manually.
- **Nocturne design** — the app wears the Nocturne identity from a Claude Design handoff: Inter type, a deep indigo-violet dark theme (the hero) and a "Daylight blue" light theme, depth via glow and layering. Home centers on a record orb ("Tap to record — meetings stay on this phone", or "some processing may leave this phone" when your settings send audio or text out) over a gradient hairline and RECENT MEETINGS cards with icon wells; recording gets a radial ground, a breathing orb glow, live EQ bars, a 44sp light-weight timer, and outlined pill controls; the meeting page has Summary | Transcript segmented tabs with a staggered reveal after generating; speakers appear as tinted chips throughout. A sun/moon toggle in the home header flips themes instantly (System/Light/Dark also in Settings). Every screen draws edge-to-edge, with the status and navigation bars transparent over the app background.
- **Background-safe recording** — capture and transcription run in a foreground service with an ongoing notification (Pause, Mark moment and Finish actions), so microphone recording continues when you switch apps or lock the phone. Device audio is the exception: on Android 15 and later, Android stops device-audio capture when the screen locks or times out, so keep the phone unlocked with the screen on for the whole meeting. Recap warns about this before asking for screen-capture consent, and if capture stops anyway, the notification and recording screen say so and what was captured up to then is kept when you tap Finish. The meeting is saved incrementally, so an interrupted session (a crash or the process being killed) is recovered the next time Recap's home screen opens, and a recovered audio-only recording has its transcript queued as Finish would have. A recovered live recording keeps the lines saved up to the interruption (see Notes and limitations). If microphone capture stops on its own mid-meeting (the microphone cannot be opened, a read fails or it stops delivering sound), the notification and the recording screen say "Recording stopped", the clock stops and only Finish is left; what was captured is kept. Finish waits for the audio file to close (3 s at most) before it queues the work that reads the recording (the first transcript, an accuracy check), so even a recording of a few seconds is transcribed. Swiping Recap away during a recording is a Finish: the last words are transcribed first (15 s at most, as at Finish, or up to a minute while the live model is still loading), then the same work is started or queued before the recording notification goes. Until then the notification reads "Finishing transcription…" with its clock stopped and no buttons; tapping it shows the recording screen while the meeting is saved, and opens Recap once it is, never a new recording. Tapping the Quick Settings tile, the widget or a calendar nudge during a recording opens the recording screen already running instead of a second one. System sounds Recap muted for a recording (see Notes and limitations) are put back even if the process dies: Recap notes which streams it muted (never ones you had muted yourself) and restores any left muted at the next recording or when Recap next opens on screen, within 24 hours; it puts the ringer mode back only while it is still where the mute left it.
- **Android 16 Live Updates** — on Android 16, while you record, Recap's notification is a Live Update: a chip in the status bar runs the recording's time, or reads Paused, No mic (the microphone carries no signal, usually because another app took it), Ended (Android ended device-audio capture) or Saving (after Finish), and the card heads the notification shade, the lock screen and the always-on display, with Pause, Mark moment and Finish.
  - Work you start yourself is a Live Update too, with a progress bar and a percentage chip: importing files (a batch of up to 10 files shows one segment per file, and the bar runs across the whole batch), Re-transcribe, Check transcript accuracy and Continue transcription started from a meeting (also when they waited behind other work, or came back through Yes to "Resume interrupted work?"), Clear voice, Remove a passage and model downloads.
  - Automatic work stays an ordinary notification: the check after a recording, the transcript of an audio-only recording, anything that waited for a charger, storage clean-up, and summaries. Media playback never is one (Android does not allow a media notification to be). Re-time words, though you start it, keeps an ordinary progress notification too.
  - Live Updates can be turned off for Recap in Android's notification settings for the app (the wording varies by phone). A Live Update you swipe away is not shown as one again for the rest of that recording, job, download queue or import batch; the ordinary notification comes back at its next change.
  - Lock-screen trade-off: a Live Update cannot carry a custom layout, so while Live Updates are on and the lock screen hides notification contents, the recording's lock-screen card shows "Recording in progress" and the time ("Finishing transcription…" once Finish is saving the meeting), never the meeting's title, and has no Mark button (Mark stays on the full notification, which a lock screen showing contents draws). Turning Live Updates off for Recap brings the button back from the next notification change.
  - The recording is built as a Live Update only when Android says Recap may post one and the notification qualifies; otherwise (Android 15 and earlier, Live Updates off for Recap, an early Android 16 build or a phone that would not promote it) it keeps its classic notification, lock-screen Mark button included. Jobs on Android 16 ask for promotion with Android 16's progress bar and leave the decision to the system; below Android 16 they keep the plain bar. On every Android version the transcription notification now has a lock-screen version, "Transcribing a recording", that shows only minutes and file counts, never the meeting's or file's name.
- **Backup & restore** — Settings → Data exports your whole library (meetings + photos + audio + voiceprints) to a single file you control — plain `.zip` or a passphrase-encrypted `.recapbak` (AES-256-GCM, PBKDF2 key derivation) — and restores from either. API keys are never included, and meeting data is excluded from Android's automatic device/cloud backups. Clear-voice copies are left out too, since they can be made again from the recording, which is backed up; after a restore the meeting plays its recording until a copy is made again. Meetings in Recently deleted, and the audio, photos and attachments they name, are left out of backups too. Restoring a backup leaves Recently deleted as it is: a backup's copy of a meeting that is there now is skipped with its files, and the result says how many were left there, with a button that opens Recently deleted.
  - **Restoring onto a phone that has some of the backup's meetings** — a restore first counts the backup's meetings that are already on the phone (a wrong passphrase is reported at this point, before anything is asked) and, when there are any, asks "Keep this phone's" (the default) or "Replace". Replace brings back the backup's copies and loses everything changed on the phone since the backup was made: notes, summaries, ticked action items, renames, speaker names and transcript edits. A kept meeting keeps its own audio, photos and attachments, and nothing that only the backup's copy names is written for it. Meetings that are not on the phone are restored either way, and the result says how many were kept. (Before 3.22.0-beta1 the backup's copy silently replaced the phone's.)
  - **Exporting** — a file deleted or renamed while the export runs (by Remove audio, the clean-up, a passage removal, or a queued import starting) is left out instead of failing the backup, and imports still waiting in the queue are not backed up. A failed export is not finished as an archive: its file is deleted, or the message says it is incomplete and should be deleted. The result shows even after Settings has closed. The default file names are `recap-backup.zip` and `recap-backup.recapbak` (`meetily-backup.*` before 3.22.0-beta1).
  - **Encrypted export** — the save picker comes first, then the passphrase, twice (at least 6 characters). One that is too short or does not match keeps the dialog open with an error on the field. Cancel, Back or a tap outside deletes the empty file the picker created, or says to delete it if the storage provider refuses, and after a rotation the passphrase is asked for again.
- **Security hardening** — optional **app lock** (fingerprint / face / device PIN required to open the app after it has been in the background for more than 30 seconds; the lock screen has no slide-in animation, so it covers the app from its own first frame, though the screen being reopened can show for a moment before it, with or without screenshot blocking; Back on it sends Recap to the background, or, when a share, Open with or Android's Manage space opened Recap over another app, returns to that app without leaving it covered; if the phone's screen lock is removed, the lock screen stands down instead of locking you out; app lock alone does not blank the Recents preview, which screenshot blocking does), optional **screenshot & recents-preview blocking** (FLAG_SECURE), and **Local-only AI** (on by default): the app refuses AI endpoints outside localhost / your LAN / Tailscale-style private ranges until you explicitly allow cloud providers, and plain-http endpoints are never allowed beyond the private network at all. Model downloads are https-only.
- **First-run onboarding with a "Set up on-device AI" step, recording-consent notice, an in-app privacy policy and Terms of use** (Settings → Privacy policy, Settings → Terms of use). The setup step suggests a transcription model and an on-device summary model that suit the phone's RAM, processor and language, and shows each one's download size and memory fit; its choices are Download both, Transcription only and Choose later. It warns before downloading over mobile data, says so when the phone is offline, and goes through the usual licence acceptance and free-space check. Choose later changes nothing. Download both turns on-device AI summaries on once the summary model has downloaded; on a phone with too little memory or free space for a summary model it offers transcription only, and the built-in offline summariser carries on. The Terms of use include a "Model use restrictions" section that incorporates the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy, the Health AI Developer Foundations terms and Prohibited Use Policy, and the Llama 3.2 and 3.1 Acceptable Use Policies.
- **Rich notes** — type plain notes during the meeting; afterwards the meeting page renders them as formatted Markdown (headings, **bold**/*italic*/`code`, bullets) with **live checklists** — tap a checkbox to toggle it right in the rendered view (refused, as editing is, while Enhance notes runs). An edit mode with a formatting toolbar (B / I / H / list / checklist) writes the markers for you; storage stays plain text, so exports and LLM context are unchanged.
- **File attachments** — attach any file (PDF, deck, doc…) to a meeting from the Files section; stored privately with the meeting, opened with the matching app on tap, included in backups, long-press to remove.
- **Attendees & speaker tagging** — list attendees for a meeting, then tag who said what three ways: tap an attendee **chip** while recording (that speaker sticks to everything said until you tap another), tap any transcript segment to tag it directly (tagging the latest segment also sets the sticky speaker), or use **Suggest speakers (AI)** on the meeting page to label untagged lines from conversational context via your LLM — always previewed, never overwriting manual tags. Speaker names flow into exports and summaries.
- **Auto-detect speakers (experimental)** — with the on-device engine, an on-device voice-embedding model (sherpa-onnx) groups transcript segments by who's talking, labeling unnamed voices "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2"…; tap any auto-labeled segment and choose "Name Speaker N everywhere…" to rename the whole voice at once. Enable in Settings → Transcription and download a speaker model (one-time, 26–109 MB: WeSpeaker ResNet34 is the recommended English default; the deeper WeSpeaker ResNet293 and ResNet152 are the most accurate; TitaNet large/small and CAM++ English+Chinese are also available). Saved voice profiles keep their enrollment audio, so when you switch speaker models they convert automatically on the next recording or import; only profiles saved without audio (from a live meeting's "Save voice", or before this feature) need re-enrolling — the app warns you which is which before switching. Manual tags always win over auto labels. (Design: DIARIZATION_PLAN.md.)
  - **CAM++** is now the re-export upstream published on 2026-11-19 (catalogue key `campplus-zh-en-v2`, 27 MB), whose embeddings no longer depend on the clip's length modulo 2 s (sherpa-onnx issue 4201). An install that already has the earlier CAM++ file keeps it, listed as "CAM++ (English + Chinese, earlier download)", and its voice profiles keep matching it; switching to the new entry converts profiles that have banked audio, as with any model switch. If the selected earlier file is missing (a restored backup, or a deleted file), the selection moves to the new entry at the next launch, which then needs its 27 MB download. New installs see only the new entry.
  - **Damaged speaker models** — sherpa-onnx's speaker-embedding loader still ends the process on a file it cannot use (v2.0.3), so Recap checks a speaker model's ONNX structure and metadata before loading it. A truncated or damaged file skips speaker detection, with a `speaker model <key> rejected: …` line in logcat (tag `SherpaEmbedder`), instead of closing the app. Parakeet and Nemotron files need no such check: since sherpa-onnx v2.0.0 their loaders report onnxruntime errors to the app, so a damaged file fails cleanly (an import shows "Could not load the … model") instead of closing it.
- **Check transcript accuracy** — the ⋮ menu on any meeting that kept its audio runs a second, heavier model over the recording and shows you exactly where the two passes disagree, moment by moment, with a play button on each one. Live capture has to keep up in real time and cuts the audio at silences; an unhurried second pass often hears more. "Both models agree" is a real answer here — it is a check, not an automatic rewrite. Keep either version per moment or swap the lot; the transcript is never touched until you say so, a cancelled check leaves it exactly as it was, and your speaker tags and highlights carry across to whatever you accept. A meeting recorded as audio only (before any transcription model was downloaded) is transcribed the same way. Turn on "Check the transcript after each meeting" (Settings → Transcription) and it runs on its own after every recording, skipping the pass when the battery is low and the phone is not charging. When the meeting has a clear-voice copy, the check can transcribe the copy instead, but only if you tick "Transcribe the clear-voice copy instead" (see Clear voice); a check of the copy leaves the recording's waveform on the Audio tab as it was. A check that fails reads "Transcript check failed" (a recording's first transcript, "Transcription failed"), not "Import failed", and one stopped by Android's daily background limit says the transcript is unchanged. In the review, a clip is refused while a recording runs, asks Android for the audio (so it does not play over a call), stops when another app takes the audio, and is prepared without freezing the screen on a long recording.
- **Flag meetings for follow-up** — tap the star on any meeting card (or use the ⋮ menu) and it stands out in the library with an accent edge; a "Flagged" chip filters the list down to just those.
- **Select several meetings** — long-press a meeting on the home list to start selecting. The header becomes a selection bar with the count, Select all, Delete and More: Add tag, Remove audio, Export as zip (Markdown), and Meeting options when exactly one meeting is selected. Tap rows to add or remove them; Back leaves selection mode; the selection survives rotation, and searching or filtering drops meetings that are no longer shown. Delete and Remove audio confirm with counts and say how many selected meetings they leave out (no audio, no transcript yet or an unfinished one, or something is working on them). Delete moves the meetings to Recently deleted, and the bar that follows has Undo. Add tag adds one tag to every selected meeting. Export writes one .zip, to a place you choose in the system file picker, with a Markdown file per meeting carrying the same fixed clinical and legal notices as a single export. Meeting options is the single-meeting sheet a long-press used to open (rename, check accuracy, summarise, topics, share, flag, with Delete set apart at the end); with TalkBack it is also a "Meeting options" action on every row, rows are announced as checkable, and the count is spoken as it changes.
- **Tags & series** — give meetings comma-separated tags on the meeting page; the home screen grows a filter-chip row with your #tags plus automatically detected recurring-meeting series ("Team standup ×5", matched by title with dates/numbers stripped). Untitled recordings, still named "Meeting <date, time>" (the date as the phone's language writes it), are not a series: they get no series chip, no remembered summary template and no pre-meeting brief match. Tags also boost "Ask your library" retrieval.
- **Weekly digest** — "Weekly digest" in the home-screen menu compiles your last 7 days: every meeting with its gist, open action items grouped by owner, and starred highlights — instantly and offline; with an LLM configured it upgrades in place to a written digest (themes, decisions, follow-ups, citing meetings). Shareable as text.
- **Transcript editing** — tap any line on the meeting page for "Edit text…" (fix transcription errors; clearing the text deletes the line), "Split into two…" (separate overlapping speakers: place the cursor where the second voice starts, split, then tag who said the second half — which can feed their voice profile too) "Fix a word everywhere…" (Find & replace, starting from a word on that line) and, on a line with a time in the recording, "Remove passage…" (see Remove a passage). Word timings survive these edits: an edited line keeps tap-to-seek and playback follow, with only the words you changed given estimated timings, and both halves of a split keep theirs, the second half's Play from here starting at its first word's audio time. (Before 3.18.0-beta1, Edit text dropped a line's timings and Split dropped both halves'.) From 3.21.0-beta1 a line remembers when an edit estimated some of its timings, and Re-time words can measure them against the recording (next item). While a meeting is being recorded, or a transcription is filling it (an import, the first transcript of an audio-only recording, Continue transcription), its meeting page refuses line edits with a note until that finishes: Edit text, Split, Delete line, the speaker tag, Name Speaker N everywhere, the long-press highlight and applying suggested speakers (edits during Remove a passage stay allowed). A line dialog confirmed after its line changed underneath, for example because a passage was removed meanwhile, changes nothing and says so.
- **Re-time words (English)** — Edit text, Find & replace and Split keep the timings of the words they leave alone and estimate the rest from the text, so on the words you changed, tap-to-seek, follow-along highlighting, clips, subtitles and Remove a passage can be a little off. Re-time words measures a line's word timings against the meeting's recording again, on the phone, with sherpa-onnx's forced aligner and an optional English model. Only the timings change, never the words.
  - **Where it is offered**: tap a transcript line → Re-time words (also among the line's TalkBack actions), or ⋮ → Re-time edited lines for all of them at once. The line action appears on lines whose timings an edit estimated, on lines with a time in the recording but no word timings in a meeting that keeps its recording (not on a caption import's lines, which never had one), and, in a meeting that keeps its recording and whose language is English or was never recorded, on lines whose timings' origin is unknown: the recogniser's own, or an edit saved before 3.21.0-beta1, which looks the same. A line already re-timed is not offered again until an edit estimates it again. The menu item takes only lines an edit marked as estimated and lines with no word timings, never those of unknown origin, so the recogniser's timings are never redone in bulk; it appears only on a meeting with a recording and at least one such line.
  - **English only, and it needs the recording**: a meeting detected or pinned as English runs at once, and one whose language was never recorded first asks "Is this meeting in English?". Another language, or a transcript Whisper translated to English from other speech, is refused with an explanation, and so is a meeting whose audio was removed.
  - **The model**: "English MFA v3.0.0 (95,000 words)" (the Montreal Forced Aligner's English model and dictionary, CC BY 4.0, as packaged by sherpa-onnx), an optional download from the sherpa-onnx project's fa-models release on GitHub: 48,540,205 bytes (about 49 MB), about 56 MB once unpacked, after which the archive is deleted. The free-space check asks for room for both side by side (101 MiB) plus the usual 10%, and the usual memory check follows. It is offered the first time it is needed, and in Settings → Transcription → Word re-timing (English), shown whichever transcription engine is selected, which downloads it with progress and deletes it (not while it downloads or a run uses it). Re-timing does not start by itself when the download finishes; tap again. The Storage screen counts it on a "Word re-timing model" row; Delete unused models leaves it alone. Like the other downloads it is not pinned to a checksum yet. Recap unpacks the .tar.bz2 with its own bzip2 and tar readers, which write only the files the aligner needs into a staging folder and refuse links, absolute or `..` paths, device entries, duplicates and oversized files. A damaged or refused archive is deleted, so the next try downloads it again; when unpacking stops because a file could not be written (the phone ran out of space), or is stopped, the downloaded archive is kept and the next Download only unpacks it. Until then the Settings row reads "Downloaded, not unpacked yet" with the space the download takes, and its Unpack or delete… button either unpacks it, with a free-space check for the unpacked files alone (54 MiB plus 10%), or deletes it (Delete download, refused like Delete model while it downloads or a run uses it; the dialog says re-timing then needs the whole download again); Re-time words offers Unpack in place of the download, and its toast and the Settings row say the model is being unpacked (the row from 90% of the progress on, for any download), not downloaded. A kept download that is still not unpacked a week after it was downloaded is deleted the next time Settings opens, as abandoned partial downloads are.
  - **The run** is a foreground job with a progress dialog (Hide, Stop, progress announced at milestones) and a "Word re-timing" notification with Stop that names no meeting. It keeps going when you leave the screen, and the notification then gives the outcome. Results are saved after each pass through the recording, so Stop keeps the lines already measured, and each pass's lines go straight into an open meeting screen. A line you edit while it runs keeps your edit: a result goes only onto a line whose words, start and time stamp are unchanged. Another edit made on the screen meanwhile (a highlight, a speaker, a fixed word) never puts a measured line's old timings back, even when you leave the meeting before the run ends: a screen's save keeps measured timings for any line it still reads the same. On Android 15 and later it is a media-processing foreground service, sharing that type's six-hour daily allowance with transcription, Clear voice and Remove a passage; reaching the limit stops it and keeps what was measured. It is not a Live Update.
  - **What it measures**: each line from its own start to the next line's start (at most 10 s past its estimated end; the meeting's last line likewise up to 10 s past its estimated end, or to the end of the recording), with 300 ms either side; lines longer than 3 minutes are left as they are, and nearby lines share one pass through the recording. A word takes the start of its first piece the dictionary knows. Words the dictionary lacks (names, jargon, numbers) and punctuation-only words are placed between their aligned neighbours by length, and a line with no word the dictionary knows keeps its old timings. The line's own start never moves, since chapters, highlights, passages and the Continue point depend on it; a word heard just before it starts at it.
  - **Busy rules**: it does not start while a recording, transcription, summary, passage removal or another re-timing runs, while anything else works on the meeting or waits for its audio, or while an accuracy check of it waits for review. A refusal says what is in the way, and nothing starts by itself afterwards: tap again once a recording or a job has finished, review the accuracy check first (from ⋮ → Check transcript accuracy), stop a clear-voice copy from the player, or answer about interrupted work on the home screen. Queued work waits until it ends. While it runs, the meeting's audio cannot be removed, the meeting cannot be deleted or cleaned up, and no passage can be removed from it.
  - **Memory**: about 200 MB of native memory while it runs (the 48 MB acoustic model and roughly 120 MB for the parsed 204,000-line pronunciation lexicon), estimated from file sizes and sherpa-onnx's data structures, not measured on a phone. Speed has not been measured on a phone either.
- **Remove a passage** — tap a transcript line (or use its TalkBack actions) → Remove passage… to take a stretch of a meeting out for good, such as a sensitive aside. Choose the first and last line and, on lines with word timings, the word to start at and the word to end after; a status line gives the span, its length and how many lines go or are shortened. The span's lines are deleted, or shortened at a word, and its audio becomes silence with 15 ms fades just outside it, so the recording keeps its length and every other time (lines, words, highlights, chapters, the Continue point) stays where it was. Where an edge falls in a line with no word timings, the span widens to take that whole line, and the confirmation says so. Only the transcript and the audio change: text made from the transcript before is not rewritten, so the summaries (marked out of date), the title, notes, action items, Ask this meeting answers and chapter titles may still mention what was said in the passage until you generate them again, edit or delete them.
  - The confirmation lists what changes and ends with "This cannot be undone.": the time range and length, the lines deleted and shortened, highlights inside the span, the audio turned to silence, the whole recording saved again as AAC when it is another kind of audio (WAV, FLAC, MP3 and so on; a lossless original loses some quality) or mixed down to 16 kHz mono when the phone's encoder cannot take its sample rate or channels (see below), chapters moved to their first line after the span or removed when no line is left, photos taken during the passage (kept), the clear-voice copy (deleted, because it holds the passage; it can be made again from the player), the summary marked out of date with its text unchanged (every summary, when the meeting has several), that the title, notes, action items, Ask this meeting answers and chapter titles are kept as they are, and an unfinished transcript that becomes complete. Clips cut from the old recording are deleted too.
  - An unfinished transcript keeps its Continue point, unless the span runs from before it to the end of the recording; then the transcript counts as complete.
  - It runs on the phone as a foreground job, with a progress dialog (Hide, Stop) and a notification with Stop that names no meeting, and says when it is done. Stopping, a failure, too little free space (the new file's estimated size plus 50 MB) or Android's daily media-processing limit changes nothing. A meeting without audio has its lines removed only, at once.
  - It cannot start while a recording runs, while the meeting is being transcribed, checked, summarised, made clear or re-timed (Re-time words), while it has queued or interrupted work or an accuracy check waiting for review, or while another passage is being removed. Queued summaries, checks and imports wait until it ends. Background playback of the meeting stops before the new audio takes its place.
  - The new audio is AAC-LC in ADTS (.aac) at the recording's own sample rate and channels (16 kHz mono where the phone's encoder cannot take those, only after the confirmation said so: when the recording turns out to need it only once it is decoded, nothing is changed, and the meeting screen asks again or the notification says to remove the passage again) and a similar bitrate, named `<recording name>-edit1.aac`, then `-edit2.aac` and so on, so an imported mp3, m4a, wav or other file becomes .aac. The encoder's start-up delay is measured on the phone first and allowed for, so the new file lines up with the old one frame for frame. The old recording, its waveform, clips and clear-voice copy are deleted only after the meeting has been saved pointing at the new file. Work files live in `audio/passage/`, which backups leave out, and a run cut short by a crash or force-stop is settled at the next start: the meeting is either unchanged or fully changed.
  - On a meeting screen left open, skip silence does not jump over the new silence until the meeting is opened again.
- **Find & replace** — ⋮ → Find & replace… on the meeting page, or "Fix a word everywhere…" in a transcript line's tap menu and its TalkBack actions. Whole word is on and Match case off by default.   - If Android's daily background limit, a crash or force-stop, Cancel or a failure part-way stops it, the words so far are kept and the meeting shows a "This transcript is unfinished. After Recap was closed mid-transcription, the "Resume interrupted work?" prompt says where it will carry on, also for an import that had already become a meeting.

When there are lines from that point on, Continue first says how many: they are transcribed again and replaced as the transcription reaches them, edits included. If it stops again, the lines it never reached are kept, so the transcript is never left shorter than it was; they show no speaker number until they are transcribed again. Continue is refused, with a note saying why, while an accuracy check of the meeting is queued (it is started then, if nothing else is running) or waiting for review, and when the transcript changed while its question was open. Continue first takes in what is stored, so on a meeting screen left open while a pass started from it was cancelled, stopped or finished, it asks about where the words stop now, or, when there is nothing left to continue, says the transcript changed and starts nothing. A check cannot be applied while the meeting is being transcribed or summarised; it is kept until then.

A transcription that stopped before it reached any pause to carry on from can only start over: the card's button then reads Start over, and the home option "From the beginning".

If you summarised the partial transcript, its summaries are marked out of date instead of being replaced.
  - Progress reads "Continuing from 42:10 · …", and "matching voices so far" while the earlier voices are re-embedded. MP3 files are decoded from the start up to the resume point, so they take a little longer to begin.
  - An accuracy check stopped by unplugging also carries on from where it stopped the next time the phone charges; one stopped by Cancel, the time limit or an error still starts again from the beginning. Accepting a check clears an unfinished first pass's resume point.

- **Auto-titling** — meetings left with the default title get named from their content after recording (instant on-device heuristic, refined by the LLM when configured).
- **On-device AI (embedded)** — Settings → Summaries → engine "On-device" runs an LLM (llama.cpp v1.0.0, GGUF) entirely on the phone: summaries, Ask, digest, topics, and speaker suggestions with zero network involvement once the model is downloaded. Fifteen models from 0.5 GB to 5.7 GB, with the estimated smallest phone each runs on: Qwen 2.5 0.5B (2 GB of RAM), Gemma 3 1B (3 GB), Qwen 2.5 1.5B (4 GB; the recommended default), Llama 3.2 3B and Gemma 3 4B (6 GB), Gemma 4 E2B (8 GB), Gemma 4 E4B, Mistral 7B and Llama 3.1 8B (12 GB) and Qwen 3.5 9B (16 GB); plus a domain specialist — **MedGemma 4B** (Google's medical Gemma tune, strong on clinical vocabulary; 6 GB). Labels state the requirement, and the picker's fit badges carry the same figures as estimates. Domain summaries (clinical and legal templates alike) are drafts, not clinical or legal documents. Reasoning models' think-aloud blocks are stripped automatically. Slower than a server; long meetings are handled with a map-reduce pass — the transcript is condensed section-by-section on-device, then summarized from the ordered notes, so hour-long meetings get full coverage (at the cost of a few extra minutes of compute). Quality sits below big cloud models, privacy sits above everything.

Answers are the same and memory use is lower; a follow-up Ask question may re-read the meeting instead of reusing the previous prompt, which is slower but never wrong.
  - **Meeting text cannot forge chat-control tokens** — on-device, text from a meeting (transcript, imported file, text read from photos, notes, titles, attendee names, your questions) is tokenised as plain text, so it can no longer act as the model's chat-control tokens (`<|im_end|>`, `<end_of_turn>`, `<|eot_id|>`, `<|turn>` and the like), and the internal message separators (U+001E/U+001F) inside it can no longer start a forged message. This hardens on-device summaries and Ask against prompt injection from meeting content (it does not stop plain-language instructions written into a meeting, which the model may still follow). It does not apply to a remote AI endpoint, which builds its own prompts from the text it receives.

## Getting the APK

Every push touching `android/` builds an APK via GitHub Actions (`Build Android APK` workflow). Download the `meetily-android-apk` artifact from the workflow run, unzip it, and install `app-release.apk` on your phone (you'll need to allow "install unknown apps" for your browser/file manager).

When the repository's `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_*` secrets are set, the APK is signed with the upload key, so every build carries the same certificate and installs over the previous one. Without them it falls back to a throwaway debug key that changes on every run, so each build has to be installed fresh (see PLAY_RELEASE.md §1). As Android developer verification rolls out (globally in 2027), APKs installed outside Play on certified devices need their signing key registered, so the upload key has to be registered for `com.recap.mobile` in Play Console (see the owner checklist in PLAY_RELEASE.md).

## Building locally

Requirements: JDK 17+, Android SDK Platform 36 (compileSdk/targetSdk 36) and Build-Tools 35.0.0, NDK 27 + CMake 3.22 (installed automatically by the Android Gradle Plugin; the pinned NDK is 27.0.12077973 and CMake is 3.22.1). The build uses AGP 8.10.1 with the Gradle 8.14.3 wrapper and Kotlin 2.0.21. The native module fetches llama.cpp v1.0.0 and whisper.cpp b5907 (release tarballs fetched by tag, with no content hash until `URL_HASH` is added to their `FetchContent_Declare` calls, an owner item in PLAY_RELEASE.md §11; both embed the released ggml 1.0.0, and whisper.cpp has no release tag on it yet, hence the build tag) at CMake configure time, and on arm64 ggml also downloads Arm KleidiAI v1.32.0 (Apache-2.0; URL and MD5 pinned by ggml) for both the `:app` and `:native-v82` builds, so the first build needs network access to github.com. CI keeps an MD5-checked KleidiAI copy in `actions/cache` and passes it in with the `kleidiaiSourceDir` Gradle property; locally, `-PkleidiaiSourceDir=<extracted kleidiai-v1.32.0 directory>` does the same (ggml's MD5 check is then skipped, so check the tarball as the workflows do).

whisper.cpp, llama.cpp, llama-common, the Silero speech gate and one ggml (with KleidiAI on arm64) are linked, with one static libc++, into one native library per CPU level: `libmeetily_native.so` (plain armv8-a on arm64, and x86_64) and `libmeetily_native_v82.so` (armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16, arm64 only, built by `:native-v82`). Until Batch 5 these were two libraries per level, `libmeetily_whisper` and `libmeetily_llama`, each with its own ggml and libc++; the compile flags per level are unchanged. `NativeEngines` loads the `_v82` build when every core has dot-product and half-precision instructions, and falls back to the baseline if it does not load.

sherpa-onnx (Parakeet, Nemotron, speaker models, the Clear voice denoiser and the Re-time words forced aligner) is not built here: `scripts/fetch_sherpa_libs.sh` downloads the v2.0.3 Android release (which bundles ONNX Runtime 1.28.2; it was v1.13.4 with 1.27.0 before Batch 5), checks the tarball against the SHA-256 pinned in the script, and installs `libsherpa-onnx-jni.so` and `libonnxruntime.so` for arm64-v8a and x86_64 into the gitignored `app/src/main/jniLibs`. CI then runs `scripts/check_sherpa_jni.py`, which checks that every native the vendored Kotlin API declares is exported on both ABIs, that both libraries are 16 KB aligned, that they need nothing beyond the system libraries and each other, and that the version stamp matches the pin. The move to v2.0.3 grows arm64 native code by about 1.2 MB uncompressed (`libsherpa-onnx-jni.so` 4,710,728 → 5,386,920 bytes, `libonnxruntime.so` 21,688,912 → 22,249,560); on x86_64 the pair is 5,913,864 + 25,581,120 bytes. See ANDROID_LOCAL_DEV.md for the local steps.

```bash
cd android
bash scripts/fetch_sherpa_libs.sh && python3 scripts/check_sherpa_jni.py   # sherpa-onnx prebuilts, checked
./gradlew testReleaseUnitTest   # pure-logic unit tests
./gradlew assembleRelease       # sideload APK (upload key if configured, else debug key)
python3 scripts/check_jni_exports.py app/build/outputs/apk/release/*.apk   # the native library exports only its Java_ entry points
```

The native library (both CPU builds) is linked with a version script (`app/src/main/cpp/jni_exports.map`) that exports only its `Java_` entry points. CI checks the built APK/AAB against `app/src/main/cpp/jni_exports.txt`, which NativeExportsTest keeps in step with the Kotlin natives. Adding or renaming a native means updating that list; the test prints the corrected file. The vendored sherpa-onnx forced-aligner API (6 Kotlin files from v2.0.3) adds 5 natives to `scripts/sherpa_jni_natives.txt`, which `check_sherpa_jni.py` checks like the rest; its manual `--jni-src` audit covers `forced-aligner.cc` too.

`BaselineProfileTest` keeps `app/src/main/baseline-prof.txt` matching the code: every line parses, and every app class and method it names, and every Kotlin standard-library method, still exists. If it fails after a rename because a rule matches nothing, update the rule (AGP would drop it silently). CI then runs `scripts/check_baseline_profile.py --self-test` and `scripts/check_baseline_profile.py` on the release APKs, which fails an APK whose profile files are missing or malformed, do not match its dex files, or lack the app's own start-up rules.

## Releasing (signed, Play-ready)

Without a keystore, the sideload APK from `assembleRelease` is signed with the debug key. For a real release, provide an upload keystore — the build picks it up automatically:

- **Locally:** create `android/keystore.properties` with `storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias`, `keyPassword` (this file is gitignored), then run `./gradlew bundleRelease` for a signed `.aab`.
- **CI:** the `Release Android (signed AAB)` workflow builds a signed AAB + APK from repository secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS`, `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. Start it by pushing a tag `android-v<versionName>` (for example `android-v3.22.0-beta1`) on the commit to release, from any branch; it warns if the tag does not match `versionName`. Its Run workflow button (manual dispatch) appears only once the workflow file is on the repository's default branch. Besides the JNI export check it fails the run if any arm64-v8a or x86_64 `.so` in the AAB or APKs has a LOAD segment aligned below 16 KB (`scripts/check_16kb_alignment.py`), and checks the baseline profile as the branch build does.
- **CI permissions:** `Build Android APK`'s build job is read-only and keeps no git credentials, because it runs CMake code fetched from outside the repository. Only its separate `publish` job (on the Android branch, `contents: write`) pushes `android/dist/meetily-android.apk` and creates the GitHub prerelease, tagged with the built commit's versionName; the notes say whether the APK has the upload key or a throwaway debug key.

When no keystore is configured the release build falls back to the debug key, so day-to-day CI stays green without secrets.

## Notes and limitations

- With the opt-in Android speech service engine, speech recognition uses the device's recognition service (Google's on most phones). Continuous listening is implemented by restarting the recognizer between utterances, so an occasional word can be missed at segment boundaries. The recognizer's start/stop chime is muted during recording by default (Settings → "Mute recognizer chime while recording"); turn that off if you need phone audio (e.g. a call on speaker) to stay audible while recording. On Android 17 an app cannot change volume or mute from the background, so Recap restores those sounds while it is still in the foreground, and puts back any a crash left muted at the next recording or app start (see Background-safe recording).
- The app is in English only. On Android 13 and later, Settings → Apps → Recap → Language offers English (United States) and the system default, from a hand-written `res/xml/locale_config.xml` (not AGP's generated one, whose `android:defaultLocale` crashes some Android 15 and 16 builds for users with none of the listed languages). The text of the exports (Markdown, PDF, Word, calendar, the library export and shared text) and the remaining status and error fallbacks now come from string resources, ready for translation; a translation adds its `values-xx` folder and its `<locale>` to that file (LocaleConfigTest checks both).
- On-device (offline) recognition requires Android 12+ and the language pack downloaded in system settings; otherwise the device's default (usually networked) recognizer is used.
- Meeting audio is kept only with the on-device engine (toggleable); the system-engine path stores transcription only. With "Keep meeting audio" off, a recording made to be transcribed after the meeting is kept until its transcript is complete, then removed (see Meeting audio).
- **End of a recording and recovery (3.22.0-beta1):**
  - Finish, or swiping Recap away, while the live model is still loading waits for the load to end: up to a minute after Finish, with "Finishing transcription…" showing. No new recording can start until then. If the load is still running after that minute, the meeting is saved as 3.21.0-beta1 did. With "Keep meeting audio" on, its audio is transcribed afterwards, possibly beside the model still loading (two models in memory). With it off, a load that then fails leaves the meeting with no words and no audio, and one that succeeds adds its words afterwards.
  - With "Keep meeting audio" off, audio captured while the live model loads is held in memory for up to 90 s. If the model fails after more than 90 s of recording, the file has the first 90 s, then everything from the failure on. Marks after that gap are offset by it.
  - With "Keep meeting audio" off and live transcription slower than speech, words still waiting 15 s after Finish are transcribed afterwards and added to the meeting (up to 10 minutes of speech). The live model stays loaded until then, and this only happens while Recap stays in memory. The automatic summary is made without those words and is marked out of date when they arrive. (As in 3.21.0-beta1.)
  - After Finish, the live model is freed once the chunk it is transcribing is done. A transcription, check or summary started at Finish, or a new live recording started straight away, can hold a second model until then. Work queued while a recording ran starts when another job ends or the home screen next opens. (As in 3.21.0-beta1.)
  - Finish waits up to 3 s for the audio file to close. If closing takes longer, the meeting, its audio and its summary are saved, but its first transcript or accuracy check is not queued automatically. Choose ⋮ → Check transcript accuracy on the meeting's page.
  - A recording the process died under is recovered the next time Recap's home screen opens. A live recording keeps the lines saved up to then. Words not yet transcribed live exist only in its audio, if it was kept, and nothing is queued for them: Check transcript accuracy brings them back. If the next recording is started straight from the Quick Settings tile, the widget, the Record shortcut or a calendar nudge, it replaces the interruption marker. The interrupted meeting is still in the library with its audio, but it gets no toast and no automatic transcript; transcribe it from its page (⋮ → Check transcript accuracy). (As in 3.21.0-beta1.)
  - A star tapped before the first live line appears is held in memory until a line arrives, and is lost if the process dies first. (As in 3.21.0-beta1.)
- Capturing the other side of a phone/VoIP call is restricted by Android for third-party apps; this app transcribes what the microphone hears (in-person meetings, or calls on speaker).
- Diagnostics in logcat, for support (shape only, never prompt, reply or transcript text): `meetily_llama` logs each model load (`local model loaded (n_ctx=..., requested ..., trained ...; n_swa=..., full/small/no SWA cache)`), each prompt's size (`prompt: ... jinja=1, spans=..., guarded=...`), an info line when a message spelled a control token, and `reusing N cached prompt tokens of M` when a prompt starts like the previous one (an Ask follow-up, for example). `meetily_ggml` carries llama.cpp's warnings and errors and its load-time lines: `load_tensors: CPU_KLEIDIAI model buffer size = ... MiB` (or `CPU_REPACK`/`CPU`), the KV and compute buffer sizes, the KV cache's cell counts and KleidiAI's kernel choice (`kleidiai: primary q8 kernel feature DOTPROD` or `I8MM`, `kleidiai: SME disabled`). Since the engines share one library and one ggml, it also carries whisper.cpp's own warnings and errors (model file problems, a prompt trimmed to fit, a language code), and KleidiAI's kernel-choice line appears once per process, at whichever engine's first model or speech-gate load comes first, so it may come with a Whisper or speech-gate load rather than the LLM's. `meetily_native` logs `loaded libmeetily_native_v82.so` (or `libmeetily_native.so`) once per process, `libmeetily_native_v82.so did not load: ...` when the fast build is skipped, and `native engines unavailable: ...` once if neither loads. `meetily_whisper` logs `whisper_full: N encoder pass(es)`, which reads 2 on a call that auto-detects the language (the first of a file: that pass always existed, and whisper.cpp b5907 now reports it), and `VAD model loaded`; `meetily_vad` logs the speech detector's one "speech gate off" warning if it cannot run. Stop also takes effect during Whisper's language detection now. `SherpaEmbedder` logs `speaker model <key> rejected: ...` when a speaker model fails the structure check, and `meetily_clearvoice` one `clear voice: ...` warning per kind of problem when a clear-voice copy cannot be made (the bundled model's copy, load or run, or the encoder). To see them all: `adb logcat -s meetily_native meetily_ggml meetily_llama meetily_whisper meetily_vad meetily_clearvoice SherpaEmbedder`.
- Cleartext traffic is configured by `res/xml/network_security_config.xml`, not `usesCleartextTraffic`. Cleartext is allowed by default only so private-network AI endpoints (e.g. Ollama on your LAN) work, and EndpointGuard enforces that in code by refusing plain http to any public address; model downloads are https-only in code (every redirect included) and in the config.
